# First-Wave Offer-Validation Defaults (full volume)

**The first wave runs at full volume (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; spec `outbound/reference/working-docs/_AARON-PROCESS-ALIGNMENT-2026-09-22.md`).** The whole qualified TAM goes out, and while `offer_market_proven` is false the offer test runs inside that full send: several offers, each in every first-wave segment it fits (its own fit filters decide; operator decision 2026-09-23), as equal cells on shared ground, read per arm, with the winner taking the rest of the TAM as soon as it is picked. Eric's three metrics and his contacts-per-positive bands remain the read, with `meeting_book_rate` reported beside them. This supersedes the "small, focused" first wave, the rule that sized the test list to the number of offers (operator decision 2026-09-22), and the sequence-length-by-TAM and Tuesday-to-Thursday defaults; the retired version is archived at `outbound/_archive/aaron-process-alignment-2026-09-23/first-wave-eric-defaults--pre-change.md`.

The `offer_market_proven` flag controls campaign strategy and the flip criterion. It no longer selects a different Email 1 copy shape. Framework A in `outreach-copy-framework.md` controls the initial cold touch in every wave.

Everything here is keyed off the `offer_market_proven` boolean, never off a wave number. This matters: `/campaign-strategy` already uses "Wave 1" to mean the *coverage* wave that runs *after* validation (for a new client, once the winner is picked). So "first wave" in the operator's sense is the **Offer-Validation Wave**, and it is selected by `offer_market_proven = false`, which also means a returning client with an already-proven offer correctly skips straight to Proven-mode.

The read (three metrics, contacts-per-positive bands) is Eric's model. The wave's shape is now Aaron's: the offer is tested inside the full-volume send, not before it (Sep 21: the whole mapped TAM of one vertical, about 96,000 sends in one week). The `offer-validation-first` order survives as the order of what is tested (the offer, then its positioning, then CTA and delivery, subject lines last), and signal slices still wait for a proven offer, but it no longer holds volume back (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes the first wave "not to scale" reading of the offer-validation-first HARD RULE, 2026-07-04). The full Eric corpus **is** carried in this workspace as of 2026-09-18: `/ask-eric` holds it, and his deliverability and vendor-benchmarking doctrine is encoded in `eric-deliverability-doctrine.md`. Route there for anything this file does not cover. (An earlier version of this line said the corpus was absent and told skills not to route to it. That was true when written and is now wrong.)

## Eric-mode copy defaults (the actual rules for the validation wave)

Apply these when `offer_market_proven = false`. Everything also passes `outreach-copy-framework.md`, `spam-word-checker.md`, and `cold-email-copy-best-practices.md`.

1. **Length and structure: Framework A controls (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21; doctrine §9, ledger R07).** "Framework A" is this system's house name for Aaron's measured winner shape from his 1.5M-email study (his own letters are not stable across videos, doctrine §0). Email 1 is 25 to 35 body words by default, 2 to 3 sentences, one to two lines plus an optional PS, with one primary ask (one CTA question by default, never stacked asks). It opens straight on the specific free deliverable: no pain, no observation, no preamble. It shows the sender/prospect workload split (the three to four real tasks we handle plus the one small thing they do, in parentheses or in prose) whenever that reflects the real process, and never invents one. Floor: never so short it reads like a bump. A longer version runs only as a logged A/B arm for a solution-aware or saturated market.
2. **Copy is offer-led, not personalization-led.** The default is the broad value-prop read of each offer. The Creative Ideas Campaign ("if I were in your shoes, here are 3 ways I'd use this," each idea constrained to a real capability of the client's product (Capability Truth governs claims about the client, not which free front-end work may be offered)) is a positioning of one offer, so it now runs in the positioning phase after the winner pick, not in this wave (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22). Under Framework A the three ideas travel with the deliverable, sent after the yes, rather than sitting as bullets in the body (`/campaign-strategy` Required Campaign Types). Minimal or no per-contact personalization. The offer carries the email. Personalization is a Proven-mode multiplier, never the thing under test here.
3. **Sequence: two emails by default at every TAM size; a third only in a genuinely small market (roughly under 3,000 qualified contacts, a house threshold) or as a tested arm (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes the sequence-length-by-TAM table, including its UNRESOLVED 1-email case flagged 2026-09-18).** Cadence Day 0 and Day 3; when a third runs, Day 0, 3, 6. Aaron runs two emails, three at most (Sep 21: step-one variants plus one step two on a 19,600-lead TAM). Email 1 carries the load; do not pile on touches to validate an offer. The capacity logic still holds in every wave: every follow-up consumes the same daily send capacity as a first touch, so a short sequence buys more first-touch reach per day from the warmed fleet, which is why the default stays at two. The retired table (1 email for a huge TAM, 2 for a mid TAM, 3 under ~50k) is archived in `outbound/_archive/aaron-process-alignment-2026-09-23/first-wave-eric-defaults--pre-change.md`.
4. **P.S. is optional, does one job, and is never a second pitch.** Under Framework A it carries either the relevance demoted from line one (the opening-line swap, examples §1.1), one same-mechanism near-twin proof point in plain numbers (examples §6), or a casual risk-elimination or reason-it's-free line (examples §5), and it keeps the email inside the Framework A length default.
5. **Proof by recognizability.** Name a proof client only when the reader will recognize it; when they will not, describe the outcome generically instead of naming a company they cannot place. This sharpens the existing "omit irrelevant proof" rule. Still bound by the Proof-Asset Gate and Capability Truth. Aaron's limits ride with it: one proof point at most, in the PS or email two, never in lines one or two, a same-mechanism near-twin with a plain legible number (doctrine §9, examples §6). Describing an unrecognizable twin by its type is fine; a claim that fits anyone ("we've worked with hundreds of clients" `[ANTI]`, examples §6) is not.
6. **Never write "I saw you're hiring for X"** (or any bare signal-naming opener). It is table stakes and gets the sender written off. If a hiring or similar signal informs the angle, use the underlying pain it implies, never the signal by name. (This is the implication test, applied to the one signal operators most often botch.)
7. **CTA: one primary ask, a soft permission question by default (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21).** The default is "Would that be worth sharing more?" `[AS]` with his variants (examples §4), and never stacked asks (doctrine §9: "Give one primary action"). The statement-only `Just reply "Yes"...` opt-in is reserved for later steps.
8. **Subject line:** 2 to 4 words, all lowercase, 0 to 2 variables (Aaron says "one to two variables" and also shows zero-variable subjects; operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21), curiosity plus relevance, never deceptive (no "FW:", fake thread, emergency, invoice or account fault; doctrine §9). Engineer the subject and preview text together for phone rendering. Subject lines are the lowest-leverage lever, so they are tested last (`/experiment-design`).
9. **Send Monday to Friday** (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes Eric's Tuesday-to-Thursday, drop-Friday default). Tue to Thu only as a tested variant or for a genuinely tiny TAM. Three send days instead of five cut weekly capacity by about 40%, which stretches the runway of a wave sized to a one-week pace.
10. **Volume: the whole qualified TAM at full volume (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes "size the test list to the number of offers", operator decision 2026-09-22, and sizing the wave to the ~300-send read floor).** Cold email is a volume game, and the first wave sends every `send_eligible` contact, P1 to P5 in score order. The offer count follows the list: `max(3, floor(qualified_contacts / 2000))` offers, capped at the qualifying candidates (Aaron, Apr 6: about one offer per 2,000 leads; May 4: always test three or four), which gives each arm about 2,000 or more first touches once the qualified TAM reaches 6,000+ contacts. The volume lives in the big campaigns (the lookalike core, the widened rows, the sold-to cuts); niche slices are legitimately small. The ~300 first-touch sends is only the raw-reply readability floor; the offer read is per arm at ~1,300 pooled contacts. If the enriched TAM cannot give three arms their ~1,300 each or each offer cell its 500 companies (a genuinely small niche), that is an ICP/offer decision to surface (widen titles / the buying committee, headcount, geography, adjacent verticals, the lookalike expansion ladder, or accept a small-niche read), never a reason to launch a pile of tiny slices or drop offers. Sending capacity is requested (`run_state.capacity_request`, the whole TAM first-touched in about one Monday-to-Friday sending week), and the client's fleet is sized to it, never the wave to the fleet: a smaller or still-warming fleet stretches the runway. Full rule: `/campaign-strategy` Launch waves and Volume adequacy.

**Already present, do not duplicate:** the Lookalike campaign (now the case-study lookalike core, which each offer that fits it runs in) and Creative Ideas (now in the positioning phase after the winner pick), offer-validation-first sequencing (now of what is tested, not of volume), minimal per-contact personalization, broad/focused/niche tiering, Capability Truth, Proof-Asset gates, and the Framework A copy gate. The phase flag does not override the cross-channel initial-copy rule.

### Sold-to IS the message (not a contradiction with "minimal personalization")

Two different layers, easy to conflate. Eric holds both at once, and so does `/campaign-strategy`.

- **Sold-to is a MESSAGE-segmentation axis.** Who a company sells to (legal teams vs restaurant owners) changes the buyer, the pain, and the proof, so it changes the message. Each sold-to segment gets its OWN copy and proof. This is Eric's "the list is the message," and it is the validation wave's PRIMARY message-differentiation (the flagship message-differential axis in `/campaign-strategy`). It is fully alive in Eric-mode.
- **"Hold copy constant" is a DIFFERENT axis, and it belongs to the coverage wave's SIGNAL-TEST slices.** There you vary only the SIGNAL and hold the copy constant so a reply-rate difference attributes to the list, not the copy. That constancy is for signal attribution; it does not apply to sold-to, it is not a validation-wave rule, and it does not freeze the big campaigns, which carry the winner's positioning variants from the winner pick on (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22).
- **"Minimal personalization" means minimal PER-CONTACT personalization** (no per-prospect AI first lines), NOT one message for the whole wave. Segment-level message differentiation by sold-to is segmentation, not personalization, and it stays. Per-contact personalization is the wave-2/3 multiplier.

So the validation wave holds the OFFER constant within each cell (the offer varies only across the offer cells inside each first-wave segment, where everything else is held equal, and each offer meets every segment it fits), differentiates the MESSAGE by sold-to segment (each with its own pain and proof), and keeps per-contact personalization minimal. Eric holds "the list is the message" and "hold copy constant to isolate a signal" simultaneously because they act on different axes: `/campaign-strategy`'s message-differential split test governs which slices get their own copy (sold-to does), while the ≥500-company floor governs which slices launch. Nothing here overrides that.

## The flip criterion: what turns `offer_market_proven` to true (or kills the offer)

The whole phase gate is inert without a concrete exit. Eric-mode does not end on a hunch; it ends on a number.

**Minimum sample, and pool within each arm (corrected 2026-07-17; per arm since 2026-09-21; read window and winner-pick retirement since the Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22).** Judge the flip **pooled within each arm**, never across arms and never per campaign: each arm is one offer, so pool that arm's first-touch contacts into one count `n` (every arm's pool is its cells across every first-wave segment it runs in, the primary's included) and its positive replies into one count `k` (a positive from any sequence step). **Read window: floor first, then sequence plus grace.** An arm (or an in-campaign copy variant) is read once it has reached its ~1,300 first-touch floor AND its last contacts have had the 2-email sequence plus a few days of grace (about 7 to 10 days from their first touch). Never read early because a window passed (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes the fixed ~21-day window). Read daily for the first week after launch. The old "~300 first-touch sends per campaign" floor was borrowed from `/cold-email-weekly-rhythm`, where 300 gates the ~1 to 3% **raw** reply rate; a **positive** reply at the 1-per-400 band is 4 to 12 times rarer, so 300 sends decides nothing (a good offer shows zero positives ~47% of the time, a coin toss). Use three states instead: **flip PROVEN** when `k >= 2 + n/660` (a strong 1-per-400 offer only reaches this on average at about 2,000 pooled contacts; the offer-count formula, `max(3, floor(qualified_contacts / 2000))`, gives each arm about that once the qualified TAM reaches 6,000+ contacts and the offers fit the whole list, and the list is never sized to the offers, which supersedes "size the test list to the number of offers", operator decision 2026-09-22); **flip DEAD** when `k <= n/660 - 2` (unreachable below ~1,300, so never call an arm dead under ~1,300 pooled contacts); **HOLD and keep sending** in between. Among the PROVEN arms, the winner that becomes, or stays, `offer.lead_magnet` is the one that does not lose on time-agreed replies per 1,000 first touches (positive replies whose thread moves toward a time), picked only on matched cell counts on shared ground (an offer alone in a segment is judged there only against the PROVEN and DEAD lines); a PROVEN arm whose time-agreed replies are zero or clearly lag the other arms is labeled farming interest, not scaled, and reshaped to filter better rather than killed, with no numeric cut-off and the operator confirming (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21; doctrine §2 step 5, §4). **At the winner pick** (made on matched cell counts on shared ground), arms still in HOLD stop receiving new first touches, their live sequences finish, the untouched rows the winner fits go to it (rows it does not fit go to the strongest offer that fits them, and two PROVEN offers with no shared ground each scale on their own ground; operator decision 2026-09-23), and the parked arm's read is recorded as unresolved so it can return as a later test (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes 'another arm's win never retires it'). Losers are switched off by the DEAD line and by the winner pick, never by a ratio: an arm as good as the leader shows 2 or fewer positives at ~1,300 contacts about 37% of the time. Full mechanism and ready-reckoner: `/campaign-strategy` flip criterion.

**Proven (flip to true):** the winning arm is clearing Eric's contacts-per-positive-response band into at least the above-average zone, roughly **1 positive reply per 400 contacted or better** (his excellent zone is 1 per 150 or better), read relative to the client's LTV:CAC and TAM per Eric's own "benchmarks are relative" caveat. When it clears and is picked as the winner (it does not lose on time-agreed replies per 1,000 first touches against the other PROVEN arms, because a positive reply is not a booked call; doctrine §0, §4; operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21), set `run_state.offer_market_proven = true`. The winner goes to the rest of the TAM immediately (every untouched row it fits; rows it does not fit go to the strongest offer that fits them), its positioning variants start inside the big campaigns, and Proven-mode's bounded signal tests open on it. `/offer-test-slate` scale review runs as a results report to the client plus a review of `offer.qualification`: the client's capacity answer sets how many qualified yeses get the free unit per week, and the rest get the standard offer. There is no client scale-review volume gate (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes sizing the coverage portfolio to the capacity the client confirms, operator directive 2026-09-22), with one carve-out: where the client literally supplies the deliverable (their trial seats, their people, their stock), the client's confirmed capacity still caps sends for that offer.

**Dead (iterate the offer, do not fan out):** an arm past the pooled sample floor (above) and worse than roughly 1 positive per 1,200 contacted is deactivated; when every arm is dead, treat the offer as unproven. Iterate it via `/offer-intelligence` validation mode (Go / Iterate / Swap), and consider Eric's reframes: SQL to MQL, chunk up (results only) or chunk down (one sexy feature). Do NOT launch signal slices on a dead offer.

**Instrumentation caveat (must be stated, not assumed away).** This criterion needs the **positive** reply rate, not the raw reply rate. If reply categorization is off (it ships off by default on some sending platforms, and some do not categorize at all), the flip cannot be measured correctly from the platform alone. Before relying on it, turn categorization on or hand-read and tag the replies interested / not, and tag the time-agreed ones too, since the winner pick reads them (the magnet-inflation guard's sending-platform proxy, `/campaign-strategy`). Raw reply rate is a deliverability signal, not an offer-proven signal, and flipping the flag on raw replies would promote a dead offer.

## The wave read (Eric's three metrics)

Judge the validation wave on Eric's three North Stars, not open rates or spam-test scores:

1. **Reply rate** (raw, deliverability + offer + list at once; ~1% floor after 200+ sends).
2. **Contacts-per-positive-response** (the offer-proven gauge above; bands: excellent ≤150, above-average 150 to 400, average 450 to 800, below 800 to 1,200, poor >1,200, all relative to LTV:CAC and TAM).
3. **Net-new email-1 sends per day** (hold first-touch volume steady; most positive replies come from email 1).

Beside them, report **time-agreed replies per 1,000 first touches** for every arm and campaign (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21; doctrine §0, §2 step 5). It does not move the flip; it picks the winner among PROVEN arms, and a positive-reply lift with no rise in time-agreed replies is farming interest, not a win (doctrine §4). Report **`meeting_book_rate`** beside it too: meetings booked / positive replies, defined once in `outbound/reference/reply-scoring-core.md` (which classes count as positive, where bookings are counted from, and the labelled denominator assumption all live there; Aaron, Sep 21: about 25%, aiming for 30%, self-reported; Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22). It reads the reply side (speed to lead, qualification, pre-call) and is the downstream meetings signal the magnet-inflation guard asks for on asset-led copy; on its own it does not flip the flag.

A rising contacts-per-reply ratio mid-wave is an early spam / list-drift alarm. Ignore open rates (the tracking pixel trips a Gmail spam banner and drops replies, and open tracking stays off on the sending platform, so opens read 0% anyway), spam-placement tests, and platform reputation scores.

## Offer and lead-magnet posture for the validation wave

The offer and magnet are chosen upstream (`/offer-intelligence`, `/lead-magnet-brainstorm`), but Eric's lens shapes what the validation wave should be testing:

- **Test at least three offers, sized to the list (Aaron, Apr 6: about one front-end offer per 2,000 leads; May 4: always test three or four; Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes "two to four", "the primary plus at least arm 1" and the capacity-gated flight).** `/lead-magnet-brainstorm` locks a primary plus every qualifying candidate as ranked validation arms (`offer.validation_arms`, uncapped since 2026-09-22), generated across Aaron's three buckets (service-based, service-delivery-based, lead-magnet or asset-based). First-wave offer count = `max(3, floor(qualified_contacts / 2000))`, where `qualified_contacts` is the wave's send-eligible contacts, capped at the number of qualifying candidates (fewer than 3 only when fewer qualify, and the report says so). They launch together on the same Framework A skeleton, each in every first-wave segment it fits (its own fit filters decide; operator decision 2026-09-23) with shared ground split into equal cells, so within a segment the offer is the only variable; the desk-ranked primary gets no head start, and only arms beyond the count queue for a later flight. Only an arm whose yes can be honored today sends (`lead_magnet_client_ok` is `not_needed` or `yes`). Each arm is judged on its own pooled read of positive replies; among PROVEN arms the one that does not lose on time-agreed replies per 1,000 first touches scales, a PROVEN arm that farms interest is reshaped rather than scaled, dead arms are deactivated, and HOLD arms are parked at the winner pick. An existing offer that `/offer-intelligence` validation mode rated Go needs no new arms: its first wave tests segments and data sources plus positioning variants of that offer (Aaron, Apr 13). For a client with per-buyer-type offer sets (`offer.segments`), each segment runs this wave on its own list with its own offers (operator decision 2026-09-22).
- **Easy to say yes to is the one trait every winning offer shares.** If the direct meeting ask is hard, the validation wave should test an MQL / lead-magnet framing, not an SQL ask.
- **Three AI-lead-magnet buckets** worth having on the table: knowledge (an intelligence report), micro-app (a small free tool), agentic free trial (run part of the service free as the first touch).
- **Test demand before building the magnet.** The validation wave IS that test: prove people want the thing before investing in building the full deliverable. Deliver a cheap teaser first, spend real money on the full asset only for positive responders.
