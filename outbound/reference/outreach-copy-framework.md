# GrowthFlare Framework A: Initial Cold Outreach

Source: `https://growthflare-copyframeworks-sop.netlify.app/#s1` (Framework A, Initial Offer; retrieved 2026-08-12).

## Scope

This is the controlling hard rule for the **first written cold touch** in any outreach channel: cold email, LinkedIn DM, LinkedIn connection note, SMS, X DM, or another written prospecting message.

It does not force the same skeleton onto same-thread follow-ups, warm replies, asset-delivery messages, interested-reply handling, nurture, or call scripts. Those are different jobs and keep their existing channel-specific rules.

## Hard Structure

Every initial cold message must pass all of these checks:

1. **Open with the offer.** A recipient-name greeting may come first, but the first substantive words must state the specific free deliverable. No observation, pain question, industry setup, compliment, sender introduction, or preamble comes first.
2. **Keep the body to 25 to 35 words.** Count whitespace-delimited tokens, so a hyphenated term with no spaces counts as one word. Count the greeting and optional P.S. Count neither the subject nor the signature.
3. **Use 2 to 3 main-body sentences and exactly 1 question.** The final main-body sentence is the only question and is the soft permission CTA. An optional P.S. is separate from the main-body sentence count, but it still counts toward the 35-word cap and cannot contain a question.
4. **Keep the workload split in parentheses.** Name 3 to 4 things the sender handles and the one small action the prospect takes. Use a semicolon, comma, or period inside the parentheses. Never use an em dash.
5. **End with soft permission.** Prefer `Would that be worth hearing more?`, `Open to it?`, or a concrete permission question such as `Can I send it over?`. The CTA must follow a named deliverable.
6. **Use proof only when it is true and compact.** An optional P.S. may carry one declarative proof claim aligned to the offer. It does not count as a main-body sentence, but its words count toward the same 25 to 35 word cap.
7. **Keep the offer real.** The free deliverable, sender workload, proof, and prospect action must all be accurate and fulfilable at reply scale.

## Channel Adaptation

- **Cold email:** use a 2-to-4-word lowercase subject with exactly one merge token, such as `fyi {{first_name}}`. Keep statistics, hype, and the offer out of the subject.
- **LinkedIn DM, SMS, X DM, and other subjectless channels:** omit the subject and use the same body structure.
- **Connection-note or character-capped channel:** write the shortest compliant version, starting at 25 words. If the offer, workload split, and permission question cannot fit truthfully, stop instead of deleting a required element.

## Skeleton

```text
Subject (email only): fyi {{first_name}}

{{first_name}}, we can put together a free [specific deliverable] for {{company_name}}.

(We'd [step 1], [step 2], and [step 3]; you'd only [minimal action].)

Would that be worth hearing more?

P.S. [Optional single true proof claim, only if the full body remains 25 to 35 words.]
```

## Failure Gate

Framework A has no setup paragraph to rescue a weak offer. If the message needs an explanation before the offer, the offer is not ready.

Do not switch to a pain-led or observation-led framework for the initial cold touch. Fix one of these instead:

- replace a vague offer with a named deliverable
- restore the parenthetical workload split
- narrow or change the audience so the offer is immediately relevant
- replace an unfulfillable free offer with one the sender can deliver

Do not produce sendable copy until the failure is fixed or the operator explicitly overrides this hard rule.

## Precedence

This rule overrides older outreach-copy instructions that require or allow any of the following for the initial cold touch:

- 50-to-90-word or 70-to-90-word first messages
- a signal, observation, pain question, proof line, or setup paragraph before the offer
- an initial CTA with no question, including `Just reply "Yes"...`
- omission of the parenthetical workload split
- more than one question

Deliverability, truth, capability, proof, and fulfilment gates still apply. When one of those gates conflicts with squeezing more material into 35 words, keep the safety gate and remove non-required copy. Never invent or overstate a claim to satisfy the framework.

## Mechanical QA

Before showing, saving, serializing, or uploading the first cold message, record:

- channel
- body word count: 25 to 35
- main-body sentence count, excluding optional P.S.: 2 to 3
- question-mark count: exactly 1
- first substantive content after any recipient-name greeting: specific free deliverable
- workload parenthetical: present
- sender tasks in parenthetical: 3 to 4
- prospect action in parenthetical: exactly 1 and genuinely small
- soft permission CTA: final main-body sentence
- proof and fulfilment: verified
- email subject, when applicable: 2 to 4 words, lowercase, exactly 1 merge token

Any failed row blocks the copy.
