# Aaron Shepherd: Example Bank and Rejection Bank

Part of the `/ask-aaron` corpus. Load it with `aaron-doctrine.md` every run. **His examples do more work than his rules**, and his *rejections* define his standards more precisely than either, which is why the rejection bank in section 8 is the most useful section in this file.

**Provenance key.** `[AS]` = Aaron's own written or spoken copy. `[AS-draft]` = his spoken draft or live rewrite. `[AS-client]` = a live client script he presents as his own team's work. `[AI-endorsed]` = machine-generated in his tool bake-off **and** endorsed by his verdict. `[ANTI]` = an example he presents as wrong. **Never treat a machine-generated line as his doctrine unless his verdict endorses it.** Caption artifacts are preserved; garbled terms are marked `[unclear]`.

**All performance figures here are self-reported by Aaron** about his own or his clients' campaigns. See `aaron-doctrine.md` section 0 before repeating any of them.

## 1. The model emails (his own, complete enough to copy the shape)

**1.1 The opening-line swap, his most transferable move.** `[AS]` He shows it as a before/after pair. Same facts, same offer; only the order changes.

> **Before (rejected):** "Hey first name, notice you're running static ads on Meta, so thought I'd reach out if we could ideate, script, edit, and record one free UGC ad creative for you. Would that be worth sharing more?"

> **After (his model):** "first name, if we could ideate, script, edit, and record one free UGC ad creative for you. Would that be worth sharing more? PS, thought it'd be relevant since you're currently running ads on Meta and 100% of them are static."

The relevance did not get deleted, it got **demoted to the PS**. Note also the parenthetical work split in prose ("ideate, script, edit, and record") and the single free deliverable.

**1.2 The lending email, his shortest documented winner.** `[AS-client]`, his own paraphrase. Carried *"no logos, no revenue figures, no case studies"* and **outperformed every proof-heavy version previously run for that client**.

> "we're not here to sell you anything. We just offer loans pretty frequently to people in this space. These are the types of loans that we offer. Thought there might be some synergies whether it be now or in the future worth a quick coffee chat."

> His gloss: *"That was like the whole email. Not that, obviously, but like that."*

**1.3 The time-collapse offer.** `[AS-client]`, B2B-YouTube-as-a-service. Two lines that carry three of the four value-equation terms.

> "first name if we launch your first video in the next 7 days at absolutely no cost."
> "So, we would ideate, write the script, do the editing and design the thumbnail. All you have to do is record."

Date-certain free deliverable (time delay), one prospect action (effort), free (risk). *"They know exactly what's going to happen."*

**1.4 The passive dream outcome.** `[AS-client]`, e-commerce funnel audit. His flagship demonstration of implying the outcome instead of promising it.

> Offer: share "a video, super low friction, of a full funnel account audit for a relevant e-commerce brand that's doing over 80 million in revenue." PS line: "we're doing a small batch of complimentary audits."

The 80 million stays; the promise disappears. *"instead of saying, 'Hey, you know, we're going to help you get to 80 million in revenue,' which might seem like a stretch, we're actually just providing some resources that showcase what an 80 million in revenue e-commerce brand is doing behind the scenes."*

**1.5 The test winner from his AI bake-off.** `[AI-endorsed]` Claude Code, look-alike list offer. **Self-reported: best-performing copy in the test; that tool's arm scored 12 interested at a 42.86% positive reply rate.** His verdict: *"Awesome."* / *"Killer."*

> "If we could build you a no-cost list of 100 companies that look like case study, same shape, same buying signals, would that be useful? PS, 24-hour turnaround takes you 2 minutes to skim."

**His clause-by-clause teardown of it, which is the densest copy rubric in the whole corpus.** Each clause has one job:

| Clause | The job he assigns it |
|---|---|
| "no cost" | *"no cost, it's free, doesn't cost anything to them."* |
| "100 companies that look exactly like your case study" | *"that shows the value."* |
| "same shape, same buying signals" | *"This just pushes that value forward a little bit more… it's elaboration."* |
| the PS as a whole | *"the PS line does a lot of the heavy lifting."* |
| "24-hour turnaround" | *"it shows that, hey, we're actually building this thing for you. It's not just something that we're sending to every single person. So, there's effort that's going into it."* |
| "takes you 2 minutes to skim" | *"it's easy to say yes to. You don't have to put much effort into it."* |

**The loser it beat, for contrast.** `[ANTI]` Same offer, same list, value buried behind a pain opener:

> "Spending too much time prospecting for new high-value clients, we can generate a look-alike list of 100 companies matching the profile of your case study client, open to a 2-minute skim of the CSV."

> His verdict: *"the value is still here, it's just buried."* / *"kind of sounds like a cheesy ad. And as soon as they read that, you know, they could be gone."*

**1.6 The lead-magnet one-liner for his own tool.** `[AS-draft]` Jul 13. **A demonstration, not a campaign result:** he translates the structure of the 589-reply client campaign (the client's own script is never read out in the transcript) onto an ROI calculator he built himself, saying *"if I were running the campaign for this lead magnet, this is exactly what the copy would end up looking like."*

> "First name, I just ran a test of 10 million cold emails and 50,000 plus in meta ad spend to determine the most cost-effective way to scale in 2026. Do you mind if I send it to you?"

> His gloss: *"It's short, to the point, value-oriented, zero ask, and again, it's opening that loop."*

**His industry variant, with his own truth condition** `[AS-draft]`:
> "Hey, I just ran a test of 10 million cold emails and 50k in meta ad spend to determine the most cost-effective way to scale industry type in 2026, do you mind if I send it to you?"
> *"Now, I wouldn't go lying about the industry type, but if this is actually an industry you've fulfilled for and gotten results for, and the data includes some of those within it, obviously you can include that."*

**His second variation** `[AS-draft]`. He calls it "email number two" but introduces it as another way to deliver the same value, not as a follow-up:
> "First name, just built a tool that shows you the most cost-effective way to book meetings with ideal customer. Open to testing it for free."
> His gloss: *"you're leading with value, so you built something, you know, usually that takes effort"*.

The "10 million emails and $50,000 plus" figure is what he calls *"the external reference point"*, added *"to increase the value of what it is that we're providing"*; whether it is one test or pooled data he does not say (doctrine section 3). What he sends on a yes is in section 7.3.

**1.7 The five-part free-trial script.** `[AS-client]`, his own paraphrase with placeholders, Sep 14. **Self-reported: 803 positive replies in one month from around 600,000 emails, with no call or client count given** (doctrine section 0). The product was a $100-a-month social media tool sold on a free trial; he withholds the exact offer wording (*"I won't give it away exactly"*). The five parts, in order:

1. **One-variable opener:** *"love the content you're putting out on Instagram handle."* His own flag: *"I know I'm normally against pleasantries and things like that, especially when it comes to personalization, but for some reason, this works."* (doctrine ledger R18)
2. **The offer with a question CTA** (a caption run-on, left unpunctuated): *"if we could help you get dream result would you be open to trying it out you know no cost for free for x amount of days whatever risk reversal it is that you actually have implemented"*. His note: *"line one and line two could be the email in and of itself because this is technically a CTA"*.
3. **The elaboration line,** built from objections the client really hears (*"we knew there was a few common objections that came up consistently time over time"*): *"Look, you'll actually be getting this result authentically without this thing that you're objecting about or that thing that you're objecting about."* / *"And we do it by X, Y, and Zed."* Its job: *"if you have a dream result, but it does not feel likely to be achieved, dream result means nothing, right? So you need to make that feel real."*
4. **A second CTA, as a statement:** *"let me know and I'll shoot you over the trial link."* Its job: *"it's also reinforcing the fact that this is free. There is a trial. There is a degree of risk that is reversed for the prospect and it's not something that we're requiring them to jump on a call for."*
5. **Signature, then a proof PS:** *"we've done this for X, Y, and Zed brands. They've gotten X, Y, and Zed results."* Its job: *"Basically, just showing them, look, like we've actually done this for people like you, and that dream outcome is possible if you do this."*

His model for the whole: *"it's almost like a landing page if you think about it."* Lines one and two are the headline; line three and the PS are *"that additional layer of the landing page that allows the person to do more research if they wanted more information"*. His scope, stated in the same breath as his preference: *"for me, I'm always up for short to the point emails. But we've put so much volume in front of this campaign that we know this combination is exactly what is working right now for this client's offer."* **A real campaign artifact that he paraphrases on camera** (any on-screen text is not in the transcript), so copy the structure, not the words (doctrine ledger R18, R19).

**1.8 The no-pitch curiosity email.** `[AS-client]`, Jun 8, anonymized by him: *"I anonymized the copy just a little bit, because we're still running this offer for our client"*. **Self-reported: about 580 positive replies from 94,000 emails sent in April to 63,000 unique prospects; 41 calls by his headline, over 71 by his call logs** (doctrine section 0). His summary: *"We sent an email that didn't pitch anything. No, 'we help X do Y,' no offer, no link, no book a call."* The beats, in his order:

1. **Subject:** described, never quoted: *"two to four words, all lowercase, no pitch, nothing about what we're actually offering, mostly curiosity-inducing"*, and above all *"it felt like it was specifically for them."*
2. **Belief shift:** *"At a certain point, what what you're doing, obviously this is blanked out, stops being about X and starts being about Y."* (The caption stutter is kept; "obviously this is blanked out" is his aside about the anonymized part.) His condition for painting the picture at all: *"when you've made the list that you have so refined, where you're pretty sure what you're mentioning is something that they're experiencing, it can hit."*
3. **Mirror line:** *"So, most founders in this case don't notice until X, Y, and Z time before it's too late essentially."* Its job: *"this is like how we got them to feel like they were in this situation."*
4. **What they are building:** *"So, we're building X, Y, and Z essentially to solve that problem, to solve that pain point that they're experiencing."* He calls this line *"basically the offer"*, although he also says the email had "no offer".
5. **The money line and the CTA:** *"You came up because a specific attribute about you add something that we don't have yet."* / *"Happy to share the thinking if you're open."* Between them he says *"So, we obviously chose you for a reason."* `[unclear: his gloss or a line of the email]`

Why the CTA works: *"you're implying specificity in the copy and then the CTA allows you to prove yourself. So, they're like, wait, are you sure?"* His truth condition: *"We're not lying, like they do actually fit the criteria that we're looking for for this offer. And we prove that during the next phase after they actually ask for us to explain."* The reply it was built to get: *"people ended up responding simply asking what the email was about, which is exactly where we wanted them."* What he sent next is in section 7.3, and the ruling is doctrine ledger R21.

## 2. Offer sentences, by signal and direction

He teaches a **fork** on every signal: call out what you saw, or go straight to the offer. He gives both versions of each, and rules that direct-to-offer is generally enough. See doctrine section 8.

**2.1 TikTok signal.** `[AS]`
> **Call-out:** "Notice your recent Tik Tok on the whatever company name page, the one about topic name… If we could map out a full strategy for you that would turn your Tik Tok into a top of funnel traffic source for revenue generating long-form content on YouTube, would that be worth sharing more?"
> **Direct-to-offer:** "If we could map out a full strategy for you that would turn your Tik Tok into a top of funnel traffic source for revenue generating long-form content on YouTube, would that be worth sharing more?"
> **Optional PS proof:** "PS tested this for XY and Zed brand recently. turn their YouTube into the second biggest conversion source in just over 45 days." He labels the PS optional: *"You don't need to include this."*

**2.2 Meta ad-spend signal.** `[AS]`
> **Call-out:** "notice you're scaling meta pretty hard. If [ROAS] is hitting [a ceiling], happy to run your first outbound campaign for free to show you our system could complement existing efforts. Worth sharing more." `[unclear: the caption garbles this clause as "rorowaz is hitting us healing"; context reads as "if ROAS is hitting a ceiling"]`
> **Direct-to-offer:** "if we could run your next outbound campaign completely free to show you how well it can complement existing ad strategies and reduce average cost per call, would that be worth sharing more? PS tested this for XY and Z brand who recently was scaling meta pretty hard. Pulled their average CPC from 375 to 197."

Note the discipline: the offer is outbound, not creative, so the call-out mentions only **that** they are scaling, never a specific ad. *"you only really want to call out the fact that they're currently scaling there."*

**2.3 Hiring signal.** `[AS]` The agency-instead-of-a-hire play.
> **Call-out (he flags this one as going too far):** "saw you're hiring for a social media and content lead. Smart move. But if you're open to alternatives, we rebuilt this exact role for X, Y, and Zed using our full stack creative team and it actually outperformed their last in-house hire by three times in 90 days. If you've got 15 minutes, I can walk you through a few high leverage changes that we'd make for zero cost."
> His own note: *"You don't have to go this far. You could realistically just chop it up here and then just jump into the offer right there specifically."*
> **Direct-to-offer:** "if we had a way to fill your open social media content lead role ASAP with the system that outperforms in-house hires by 3x in 90 days with the first 7 days of content free to try us out. Is that worth sharing more?"
> His gloss: *"maybe you don't need to make this hire. Maybe you can just bring us on… and we'll do it for free to show you we're good."* One sentence carrying time delay (ASAP), likelihood (3x/90 days), risk reversal (7 days free) and a soft CTA.

**2.4 The one-liner form.** `[AS-draft]` His stated spec was *"I just want this to be a one-liner truthfully."*
> "Hey, if we could send you a list of 50 companies that look exactly like X case study, would that be worth sending over?"

**2.5 The Loom-offer line.** `[AS-draft]` Written live. Stated intent: *"low friction, unique mechanism, emphasize the output."*
> "First name, if I could send you a two-minute loom video explaining how we're pulling together multiple channels to go from 10 to 20 calls per month up to 40 to 50. Would that be worth sending it over?"

**2.6 Building off momentum.** `[AS-draft]` *"I like the idea of building off of their momentum."*
> "saw that you recently closed a major client in whatever space. If we could help you find more clients like that by building out a perfect look-alike list of 100 companies at no cost, would that be helpful?"

**2.7 Good front-end offers he names.** `[AS]` "a free lead list of 50 high-intent prospects" · "a free ad creative" · "a free video" · "a free cold email campaign" · one complete YouTube video, fully produced · a no-cost look-alike list of 100 companies built from the prospect's own best case study · a 2-minute Loom teardown · [Jul 13] free interactive tools built on the prospect's own numbers: a CRO audit (*"No login, 60-second results, 100% free."*), a business valuation, and his own ROI calculator comparing outbound with Meta ads · [Sep 14] a free trial of a paid product (section 1.7).

## 3. Subject lines

**His own, endorsed** `[AS]`: "fyi first name" · "question about topic" (*"That one's always a good one."*) · "your recent tiktok" · "meta ad problem" · "your new landing page" · "Issue with your page." · "your recent GPT review"

**The spec he states:** two to four words (he says *"two-to-three-word"* and elsewhere *"three to four-word"*), **all lowercase**, curiosity plus enough relevance to imply the topic. Engineer the preview text with it: *"the first like, let's say, five, six words."* [06-08] adds the no-pitch version: *"no pitch, nothing about what we're actually offering, mostly curiosity-inducing"*, where the real test is that *"it felt like it was specifically for them."*

**Rejected subject lines, with his reasons** `[ANTI]`:
- "free creative ad strategy", *"someone who knows that they don't really care about ads isn't going to read this at all, even if your offer is fantastic."*
- "Can it be achieved?", *"they leaned too much into curiosity… you have zero clue what this is going to be about, and because it's so ambiguous, there's nothing that actually makes you want to open."*
- "{first name}, one quick question", *"they see this, they know it's a cold email… it doesn't allude to what it is that they are potentially interested in at all."*
- An "FW:" fake-forward prefix, *"almost like a little bit of a trick to make it feel like an internal email… last thing you want to do is start the conversation with deceit."* His honest substitute for the same casual effect is simply lowercase.

**The conditional on using an open role in the subject:** *"I like using the role in the subject line because they might think that you're an applicant… As long as they are the hiring person in that space. If they're not the hiring person, just go back to something a little bit more simple. Maybe don't even mention the role."*

## 4. CTAs

**His signature ask, and it is narrow on purpose** `[AS]`:
> **"Would that be worth sharing more?"**

Variants he uses: "Worth sharing more." · "Is that worth sharing more?" · "would that be worth sending over?" · "Mind if I send it to you?" · "Do you mind if I send it to you?" · "If you've got 15 minutes, I can walk you through a few high leverage changes that we'd make for zero cost." · "Open to testing it for free." `[AS-draft]` Jul 13 · "would you be open to trying it out" and then, in the same email, "let me know and I'll shoot you over the trial link" `[AS-client]` Sep 14. He calls the second *"almost like the second CTA"*; both ask for the same reply (doctrine ledger R19). · "Happy to share the thinking if you're open." `[AS-client]` Jun 8, the close of the no-pitch email (section 1.8).

**What he replaced and why** `[ANTI]`: "would that be worth a chat" / "would that be worth exploring" / "would that be worth whatever." His reason is friction, not phrasing: *"It's very rare that someone is immediately like, yeah, sure, let me just throw you 30 minutes of my time and we'll chat."* The ask is **permission to send something**, not permission to meet.

**He also tests no CTA at all.** His live A/B: *"having two lines plus a PS against something like a one-liner entirely with no PS line, no CTA, all kind of together in one."*

## 5. Risk reversal: "casual risk elimination"

He retired the guarantee himself and named the replacement. *"What really works in 2026 is basically just like casual risk elimination. Something that sounds like a reasonable person just being up front."*

**His model lines** `[AS]`:
> "We'll do this for free and then we only move forward if we actually see something worth implementing."
> "If it's not a fit, no hard feelings. I just want to get in front of you."

> *"that essentially doesn't feel like a pitch, right? It just feels like someone trying to start a conversation. And that is exactly what makes it work."*

**Why he ranks it above personalization now:** *"Personalization basically says like, 'Hey, I did some research on you.' A risk reversal says, 'I'm so confident in what I'm offering that I'm taking all of the pressure off you to get this started.' Now, that is a completely different conversation."*

**Why pay-on-result beats plain free:** *"You're actually doing it for free until you get the result and then that's when you get paid. Which again, makes people's BS radar just go off a little bit less."*

**The same idea as a trial** [Sep 14]: *"anytime you can offer some sort of trial with the risk reversal, it's just going to make it that much easier for the person to say yes."* His ads version: three creatives in their account against their best ad, and *"if they don't beat your control, they're absolutely free. You can just delete them."*

**The too-good-to-be-true elaboration** `[AS]`: acknowledge it, then justify it with the mechanism.
> "This isn't necessarily too good to be true because it's actually a mechanism that we use to find some of our best clients."

**Disqualifying with the free offer** `[AS-client]`: *"he will intentionally tell them the strategy behind doing the free creative… we're only offering this free creative to those that we think that we're going to get really, really good results for. And if you don't meet X Y and Z criteria, we're probably not going to do the free thing for you… It'll disqualify anybody that just wants the free thing."*

**Rejected** `[ANTI]`: the money-back guarantee, *"in my opinion, incredibly washed out."* And "get results in 90 days or you don't pay", because *"there is no definition of results at all."*

## 6. Proof and PS lines

**His PS social-proof template** `[AS]`:
> "Client went from X bad result to X good result using this exact process."

**Rules that travel with it:** one proof point maximum (*"if you have more than one proof point in your email, you're over justifying"*), never in line one or two, PS only, and often deferred entirely to email two: *"these PS lines not necessary… In some cases, we actually see social proof in certain areas almost just like make the message a little bit salesier. So often times we'll save it for email two."*

**Proof phrasing he refuses** `[ANTI]`: "we've worked with hundreds of clients" · "here's what our clients have to say" · "We helped so-and-so brand go from 1 mil to 10 mil in revenue in 3 years, blah blah blah blah." His verdict on the category: *"generic social proof is completely useless and I almost avoid using it most cases."*

**The acronym rule.** `[ANTI]` "one swimwear brand dropped CPAC by 76%" fails because *"as soon as you jump into really crazy acronyms and things like that, you lost them. Like people want to know that you made more money, you saved more money… I run Meta ads and I don't even know what that means."* His fix is a plain, named, legible number: "Nani Swimwear saw a 942% lift in ads to cart."

**Proof placement doctrine:** make the case a **twin** (same space, similar revenue range) and weave it into the offer. *"nowhere in this email do you see, 'We helped so-and-so brand go from 1 mil to 10 mil.' It's all very passive and weaved within the offer."*

**A dated exception: proof in email one** `[AS-client]`, Sep 14. The five-part free-trial script (section 1.7) closes on a PS naming several brands and results, *"just hammering home social proof"*. It keeps proof below the signature, but he does not say how several brands square with the one-proof-point rule (doctrine ledger R19).

## 7. Follow-ups, and what happens after a yes

### 7.1 Cold follow-up

**Sequence length, stated explicitly and with unusual force** `[AS]`, on a 5-email sequence he tears down: *"**Absolutely do not do this in 2026 at all.** You want to max it at two, maybe three messages at the absolute most… at that point you just hit the level of annoyance where they're just pissed off."* This matches his other statement of generally two emails, perhaps three in a small market.

**The standalone rule, as a good/bad pair** `[AS]`:
> **Bad:** "just following up on the above, interested in having me create that for you"
> **Good:** "interested in a free UGC ad creative"
> **Good (variant):** "interested in a 2-minute video that explains our XYZ process"

**A follow-up must switch the mechanism or strip to the core offer, never repeat email one.** His teardown of a failure: *"they basically just tried the exact same thing as they did in their initial email one, but two days later… they didn't swap up what the thing is that they were actually offering at any point. It basically just went from offering a low friction overview to a low friction walk-through, which I imagine is the same thing."*

**The de-escalation pattern (his preferred form).** The follow-up offers the *same* thing at lower friction: *"if the first email is something like offering to connect them with particular prospects in a given industry, the follow-up could be something like offering to send them a video explaining how it was done."*

**The Loom follow-up** `[AS]`, his named instance:
> "Should I send you a two-minute Loom video showing you exactly how this works?"
> Why it works: *"there's zero commitment involved and just curiosity."*

**The one cold follow-up he describes in detail** [06-08, the no-pitch email in section 1.8] carried only two ingredients: *"it builds a little bit of scarcity and then just reinforced the low friction CTA"*, re-serving the identical ask. As he reads it out: *"So again, do you want me to explain why I thought that this was the case?"* He is dismissive of the craft involved: *"the follow-up wasn't really anything special to be honest."*

**Banned follow-up moves** `[ANTI]`: "just following up" · "just nudging" · "just wanted to bump up my last message in case you didn't get a chance to read it" · empty bumps · guilt trips · fake urgency · "did you see my last email?" · **"Are you the right person to send it to?"** (*"if you built the lead list you should know that I'm the person to reach out to"*, valid only at enterprise, and only when specific: *"Hey, is David in sales better to reach out to about this?"*).

### 7.2 The post-interest subsequence (fires after a hand-raise, not cold)

His only structured sequence: *"as soon as somebody shows interest, put them into a subsequence that then reminds them to book that discovery call shortly after. This can be one, two, or three emails coming shortly after they've shown interest, whether that's two days, three days, you know, six days, maybe one after that."*

Content rule per step: *"you don't want these to be like your typical just following up or just nudging this. You actually want to show curiosity and ask them if they had the opportunity to dive into the asset and if they had any thoughts regarding it."*

**The escalation principle:** the ask stays constant across follow-ups. **Escalation happens on reply, not on a timer.**

**And he rates the phone above the emails.** Alongside the subsequence he enriches for a mobile number so a rep can *"call every single interested reply within 5 minutes."* He calls this **"the kicker."** His warm-call script `[AS]`:
> "Hey, this is so-and-so from XYZ company. You just replied to my email about so-and-so, so I just thought it'd be faster if I give you a shout and found a time for us to discuss. Like, does Tuesday at 1:00 p.m. or Wednesday at 3:00 p.m. work better for you?"

**His team's newer version** `[AS-client]`, Sep 14, from a branching call script he calls a *"situation tree"*. The setter calls every positive replier *"within like 5 minutes of the interested response coming through"*:
> "Hey, first name, this is SDR's name with client company. you answered an email about X, Y, and Z offer. Does that sound familiar at all?"
> "He's got a couple slots on his calendar. Thursday at 2 or Friday at 1 p.m. Do either of those times work for you?"
> Prequalification before booking: "Just so we make sure chat's as efficient and relevant as possible. Do you mind just answering a couple questions prior to booking this in"

No answer: *"we just leave a quick voicemail. We've already sent the email reply. You might shoot a quick text as well"*. No time: *"Give them what they need to book on their own time, including a scheduling link, the information to overcome the objection, and so on."* This call lands before the prospect can have used what they asked for, which sits against section 7.3's Jul 13 rule of no sales friction until they have consumed the value (doctrine ledger R20).

### 7.3 What he sends on a yes (read both halves, they must travel together)

**The rule:** deliver something of substance, and keep the next ask present but non-coercive. His verb for the failure mode is **"force-feed."** The quotes from here down to "the premise underneath it" come from the Jun 8 no-pitch campaign [06-08] (section 1.8): the reply asked what the email was about, and the answer was a video.

> *"We respond with the video which shares more details about what it is exactly that we're offering. Low friction CTA. We're not going to shoot another calendar link or anything. We don't want to force-feed them the calendar. It's basically like, 'Hey, calendar's on the next page if you think there's interest.'"*

**The second half, which is what people miss:** the ask is not deleted, it is moved one layer down. The VSL page itself *"included everything that you would usually try to stuff into a cold email. The offer, the outcome, the proof, the introduction to our client, exactly what they do. **All with a Calendly link to book a call at the end of it.**"*

**His reasoning (commitment bias):** *"By getting them to reply to the first email, they were already more invested in hearing us out entirely, kind of closing that loop. They had a psychological need to see the conversation through, which meant the VSL was received far better than if we tried to include it in our first message."*

**The model he names for the whole thing:** *"We basically created a sales funnel within our cold email campaign. The first message only had to get impressions like an ad would on Meta. Then, once they replied, they got to the actual pitch. And once they clicked on the video, that's when we actually pitched them."*

**And the premise underneath it:** *"the cold email isn't where you sell. It just gets the first interested reply. What comes after that is what actually does the selling. Because even if your lead list, your offer, and script are absolutely perfect, the reality is you can't build up enough trust in just a couple of sentences to sell anything."*

**Other dated variants:** the reply *is* the booking mechanism ("Just reply back to me in the email, and we'll get something scheduled in"); or deliver the pitch deck and suggest an in-person sit-down at an event, withholding detail until after interest because *"we don't want to just force-feed them a bunch of numbers if that's not really their thing."*

**His own delivery reply, for his ROI calculator** `[AS-draft]`, Jul 13. When someone says send it:
> "Great first name, here's a link to the results or tool." (he shares the link) "Curious to get your take on it."

**What he refuses to put in it** `[ANTI]`:
> "Oh, if it'd be worth 30 minutes to chat, I would love to walk you through like X, Y, and Z and how it applies to you."
> His reason: *"then you're inherently reducing the value of the thing that you're providing and you're making it very clear that there's a reason behind you sending them that value, right?"* / *"It's almost like they're thinking that they're getting caught in like a trapdoor. So, you don't want to add any sort of sales friction until they've actually consumed the value."*

**Where the call goes instead: into the asset.** The calculator's own CTA offers a free TAM analysis on a call, because *"this creates the gap. You solve the gap with the book call. So, you're not pitching the call within the messaging itself or the reply itself, but you're having the call as the natural next step to fill the gap that you're creating with the lead magnet that you're providing."*

**On over-answering a reply:** *"If a prospect writes back asking what you do and you reply with your full process, pricing structure, three case studies, you've answered everything again. There's no loop left open. No reason to get on a call. One sentence is almost always the better move."*

## 8. The rejection bank (his real standards, stated as what he kills)

This is the highest-value section in the corpus for a persona. Each row is a thing he rejected on camera, his verbatim reason, and his fix.

| What he rejected | His verbatim reason | His fix |
|---|---|---|
| "we help you get more visibility with Reddit and SEO" | *"he doesn't really explain how, doesn't explain the mechanism, doesn't explain the end result… The likelihood of achievement doesn't seem very high because there's no proof, there's no believability. It's kind of just this figurative thing."* | Named tools + named category + named deliverable + pay-on-result |
| A "poke the bear" curiosity opener | *"that was your typical poke the bear, basically trying to create intrigue before presenting the offer"* | Lead with the dream outcome directly |
| An offer only findable on a linked profile | *"the offer itself in this case, I actually had to do some digging and open up his Fiverr profile"* | State the offer in the email body |
| "five to nine qualified calls monthly" | *"there's no risk reversal and the dream outcome is severely lacking… if my ticket size isn't high enough, that five to nine calls might not even turn into a closed deal"* | "10 to 20 qualified calls with your dream client" + pay per qualified call |
| "I can help you monetize your channel, increase your watch time, and increase your targeted audience" | *"Don't know how many views they're going to increase it by, don't know what they're going to do to help me hit my target audience. **There's nothing specified at all.**"* | A free fully-produced video |
| **The word "audit"** | *"the word audit is just so overplayed… when you're doing outbound, nobody really wants an audit from somebody that they don't know… to them, **an audit means nothing. An audit means that they have to jump on a 30-minute call and waste your time.**"* | A free done-for-you deliverable |
| A "revenue leak sprint" offer needing ad-account access | *"Who are you to suggest that you know better than they do about the system that they're running? And then… having to give them access to your Meta ad manager, it's going to be a tough one to do via cold because **they don't know you, they don't like you, they don't trust you.**"* | A deliverable that needs no back-end access |
| A "feed to ad profitability snapshot" offer | *"I don't really like this as much because **it's not really transitioning well into the core offer**"* | The front-end offer must transition into the core service |
| "pinpoint which hooks actually convert" | *"nobody really knows what you're getting out of that. Like is that a call? Are you going to send me a document? Is that just going to be you replying to the email?"* | "create five ad creatives for you completely free" |
| "No retainer, just razor sharp insights you keep regardless" | *"the no retainer note kind of sounds salesy. And then again, razor sharp insights, marketing lingo."* | Cut it. State the free deliverable plainly |
| **Em dashes** | *"no m dashes ever, **that screams ChatGPT**"* | Chunk with line breaks instead |
| Picture-painting before the offer | *"I really, really don't like painting the picture in copy… when you lead off [with the offer] it's a little bit more direct, gets to the reader a lot faster, allows them to digest things and really see what the value is without the possibility of them falling off."* | Offer on line one, relevance to the PS |
| The company-name variable | *"every single person uses company name variable, and often times it's not that good"* / *"**which I actually hate**"* | The case-study variable, used as the *target* of the deliverable |
| Hook-payoff mismatch | *"it's leaning on one pain point and then using a different solution, which isn't correlated at all. Like, ideally you talk about volatile lead flow and then you talk about how multi-channel solves for volatile lead flow."* | Match the hook's pain to the offer's pain |
| Question-form pain openers | *"right away, when you're like spending too much time prospecting for high new value clients, kind of sounds like a cheesy ad. And as soon as they read that, you know, they could be gone."* | Open on the offer |
| Self-declared jargon uniqueness | *"It's pulling in stuff from my website that's super jargony, calling it unique… we want to allude to the fact that what we're offering is a unique mechanism. We don't want to say, 'Hey, this is our unique growth architecture OS.' **We can't use jargon.**"* | Allude to the mechanism in plain words |
| Long essay emails | *"they either write these long essays that nobody reads"* / *"most people aren't even going to read half of that"* | Offer-as-copy, chunked |
| A 5-email sequence | *"**Absolutely do not do this in 2026 at all.** You want to max it at two, maybe three messages at the absolute most."* | 2, max 3 |
| A follow-up repeating email one | *"they didn't swap up what the thing is that they were actually offering at any point"* | Switch mechanism, or strip to the core offer |
| "Are you the right person to send it to?" | *"truthfully if you built the lead list you should know that I'm the person to reach out to"* | Only at enterprise, and named: "is David in sales better to reach out to about this?" |
| Money-back guarantees | *"**incredibly washed out**"* | Casual risk elimination: free until the result lands |
| Personalization as the differentiator | *"that edge is completely gone. AI can pretty much personalize everything at scale now… what used to feel like a genuine human touch is actually turning into a reason for your emails to be ignored."* | Risk reversal as the differentiator; personalize the ICP variable only |
| A recognizable-token opener | "I noticed you recently expanded into enterprise.", *"a recognizable token. There's no signal of genuine understanding behind it."* | An observable trigger true for them and only them right now |
| AI copy that restates the brief | *"It's kind of just spitting out exactly what I tell it. It's not really getting creative… not pulling any information from the website like I would have hoped."* | Mine the real case studies and value props from the source |
| Omitting that a free offer is free | *"none of these prompts have actually mentioned that the look-alike list is no cost. **I think that's a huge miss.**"* | Say "free" or "no cost" explicitly |
| Talking about metrics the buyer ignores | *"I don't like talking about email rates, like nobody cares about that stuff"* | Name a pain they actually care about |
| "What if you could double revenue?" | A loaded hypothetical standing in for a deliverable | A credible concrete deliverable |
| "Still losing money to X?" without evidence | Assumes an unverified problem | A neutral question you can answer honestly either way |
| Robotic or template-shaped copy | *"kind of sounds robotic"* / *"as soon as something reads like a template that they've already read three times this month, they stop and leave, **no matter how good your offer is**"* | Sound different from what everybody else is saying |
| A call pitch inside the delivery reply, "if it'd be worth 30 minutes to chat, I would love to walk you through like X, Y, and Z" [Jul 13] | *"It's almost like they're thinking that they're getting caught in like a trapdoor. So, you don't want to add any sort of sales friction until they've actually consumed the value."* | "Great first name, here's a link to the results or tool." plus "Curious to get your take on it." (section 7.3) |
| A lead magnet with no next step [Jul 13] | *"There's so many people that create lead magnets and calculators and things like that with no natural progression to the next step. And that's why so many people say yes to them, but you have such a low meeting to lead ratio."* | Point the asset's own CTA at *"one gap that the prospect likely couldn't fill themselves"*, and let the call fill it |
| AI-generated PDFs [Jul 13] | *"Don't go sending those AI generated PDFs that aren't based on any legitimate data. That's just not something a prospect feels as useful and low friction and it just means they have to spend the next 10 minutes reading through a document that they might not even need."* | A free tool built on the prospect's own business and numbers |
| A compliment on their post, a line about what you do, and a book-a-call CTA [06-08] | *"There's some compliment on something that they posted on LinkedIn, a line about what you help people do, and then obviously a CTA to book a call. Every potential lead knows exactly what's happening, which puts their guard up even before they finish the first sentence."* | In that campaign, an email that pitched nothing at all (section 1.8). **Dated exception:** Sep 14 ships a one-variable compliment line and flags it (doctrine ledger R18) |

**His verdict vocabulary**, useful for channeling the voice: *"killer"* · *"awesome"* · *"solid"* · *"fantastic"* · *"don't hate it"* · *"pretty slim"* · *"kind of sounds robotic"* · *"not looking good so far"* · *"this is awful."*

**And his own pre-send test, which is the whole rejection bank in one line:** *"if I received that email, I definitely would not be answering it."*

## 9. Anti-pattern offers (his named weak offers)

| `[ANTI]` | Why |
|---|---|
| "We're a B2B YouTube agency that helps B2B companies grow their online presence." | A service description. *"All that really does is tell me what you do. It doesn't tell me anything about what I'll actually get."* |
| "Hey first name, if we could help you drive 80 million in revenue using our ad-based whatever system, guaranteed or you don't pay, would that be worth a chat?" | The retired old-school shape: outcome stated head-on plus a guarantee |
| "if we could help you book 10 to 20 more calls in the next 90 days or you don't pay, would that be worth a chat?" | Same, plus a 90-day window that loses to a 7-day pre-commitment win |
| "offering them 20 to 30 calls per month or they don't pay" | His named **bad offer**: a performance guarantee on the core service |
| The "headache" offer | *"we're going to grow up to $80 million a year. It's going to take all of these changes. We're going to have to implement this CRM and add this ads platform… get rid of our old vendor, bring in a new vendor. It just like **most of those offers sound like a headache**."* |
| A broad question offer that farms yeses | *"anyone can really make their offer a broad enough question to get a yes, but that doesn't mean anything if the person was never going to buy from you. All it really does is waste time on unqualified leads."* |
| An inbound or referral offer reused on strangers | His stated **#1 cold email killer**. It is the offer, and he explicitly rules out the alternatives: *"it's not their lead list, or their email scripts, or their deliverability."* |
| A commoditized offer | *"because it's been used a ton, you're probably going to have to find a layer above that to really make what you're doing sound unique versus everybody else."* |

## 10. His self-reported numbers (carry the caveats from doctrine section 0)

Recorded because a persona will be asked for them, and because the internal inconsistencies matter as much as the figures.

- **Interested rates:** *"We have tons of clients that are getting 20, 30% interested rates. It all depends on the offer."*
- **Meeting-book-rate benchmark** [06-08] for tighter, higher-friction strategies: **30 to 50%** of positive replies. He names it to contrast with his own curiosity-led campaign, which did far worse on that ratio.
- **The 587-reply campaign [06-08], his own figures:** *"we sent 94,000 cold emails on behalf of our client during the month of April, of which 63,000 were unique prospects"*, at *"around a 2% reply rate"*, half of those replies positive (*"So, 580 overall"*), and *"up to 700 interested responses"* once May is included. On sends, 41 calls in the month is about one per 2,300 emails; the "over 71" may span May, whose sends he never gives. ~580-587 positive replies → *"over 71 booked calls"* → *"about 40 to 50 ended up falling within their age criteria."* Roughly 12% reply-to-call and 7-9% reply-to-qualified-call. **He reports the same campaign's calls as 41 in the intro and closing, and "over 71" from the call logs, and never reconciles them.**
- **His explicit caveat on curiosity-led offers** [06-08, said of the no-pitch email]: *"when you really, really, really build curiosity and establish that open loop, it does get a lot of people interested that aren't necessarily going to convert."*
- **The structural tradeoff of a low-friction front end:** *"you're going to receive a ton of interested responses and you need to do a little bit more heavy lifting on the back end to actually convert interested prospects into book meetings."*
- **The non-in-market replies, reframed:** *"you're building out this database of prospects that you know are going to one day come to you… presenting the opportunity to create customers in 3 months, 6 months, 9 months, 12 months."*
- **The 240/270/323 campaign:** the title says 240, he says 270, then corrects himself to *"closer to 323."* The title number never appears in the transcript body. No meeting count is ever given for it.
- **The 803-reply campaign [Sep 14]:** *"803 positive replies"* in the intro and *"over 803 positive responses"* at the close, *"all from around 600,000 cold emails"*, which is one self-graded positive per roughly 750 sends. **No booked-call or client count is given**, although he credits the reply handling with turning them *"into actual paying clients."* He also says the script had to be simple enough to *"send 30,000 times a day"*, which over a month would exceed the 600,000 total, so read it as capacity rather than the average (this corpus's reading).
- **Career figure drift across four months:** *"over 2,000 calls from cold email in just the past year"* → *"over 3,000 sales calls"* → *"around 3,000"* → *"over 3,000… in the last year alone."* Elsewhere *"close to like 3 to 4,000."* Monthly send volume moves the same way: *"We send over 1.5 million cold emails every single month"* [Mar 16] becomes *"more than 2.5 million cold emails every single month"* [Sep 14].
- **The AI bake-off:** Claude Code's arm scored 12 interested at 42.86% positive reply rate and he named it the winner. Two other tools were both given nine interested responses but different rates and ranks, which does not reconcile.

**Do not present any of these as a benchmark the operator should expect to hit.** They are one agency's self-graded numbers, and where he contradicts himself the contradiction is part of the record.
