## What makes the STRONG example strong (the mechanics to copy)

The reference slide's strong email works because of five moves, not because it is longer:

1. **It mirrors the prospect's own world back to them first.** It opens by reflecting what the prospect's current outbound probably sounds like ("We help DTC brands scale through paid social..."), then says why that isn't landing. That is the diagnostic mirror: you prove you understand their situation before you pitch anything.
2. **It uses real, specific research about them.** "You're one of the few agencies specialising exclusively in subscription brands, you've publicly shared you've managed over $75M in ad spend, your case studies focus on LTV." Not a homepage summary. The specific facts are the personalization.
3. **It names the pain the prospect's BUYERS actually feel**, in their language ("why MER is slipping, why first-order profitability disappeared, why Meta performance plateaued"), which is what makes the sender sound like an insider, not a vendor.
4. **It is written one-to-one.** The sender speaks as "I" ("that's the conversation I'd build your outbound around"). Almost no "we/us." It reads like one person typing to one person.
5. **The ask is frictionless.** "Just reply 'Yes' and I'll shoot them across." The sender does all the work; the prospect says one word. No obligation, no catch, no "want me to."

The length (~180 words) is a RESULT of doing 1-4 honestly, not a goal. Every line is about the prospect. Copy the moves, not the word count.

## Sample sequence (4-step, two threads): performance agency to DTC / subscription brand

Scenario: the sender runs paid growth for a small number of subscription / DTC ecommerce brands and wants more of those brands as clients. Prospect: a founder or growth lead at a subscription ecommerce brand. `[Brand]`, the persona name, and every proof point are placeholders.

### Email 1, Thread 1 (new thread). Angle: the audience/LTV math, not the tired ad.

**Subject:** your meta numbers

```text
Sarah, if your Meta account looks anything like most subscription brands I see, the story usually goes: ROAS held for a while, then slid, and lately every new creative buys a week or two before it fatigues again.

Most agencies would say the fix is more creative volume. Sometimes it is. But for a subscription brand the number that breaks first usually isn't ROAS at all. It's the gap between first-order CAC and the point where LTV finally catches up. Widen that gap and no amount of new creative saves the account, because you're just buying the wrong customer faster.

That's the part I'd want to look at for [Brand]: not which ad is tired, but whether the accounts you're scaling are the ones that actually stick.

I put together a short read on where subscription brands your size lose that math, with three specific things I'd test on your account first. Just reply "Yes" and I'll send it over.

Sarah
```

Why it works: diagnostic-mirror opener (reflects their likely account story), one-to-one "I", earned length (every line diagnoses the subscription paid-social reality), names a real buyer-side pain (CAC-to-LTV gap), CTA is the easy-yes opt-in after naming a concrete deliverable.

### Email 2, Thread 1 (same thread, support). Proof + question CTA.

```text
Sarah, one number that makes this concrete: for a subscription brand, a 10% shift in which customers you acquire can move blended MER more than doubling creative output does, because retention compounds and a tired ad doesn't.

I mapped this for a skincare subscription brand last quarter, and the leak wasn't the ads at all. It was two audiences quietly pulling in one-and-done buyers.

Would it be useful to see which of your current audiences are most likely doing that?

Sarah
```

Why it works: supports Email 1 with a proof story (placeholder proof, swap for real), stays short, closes on a forward-looking QUESTION (the follow-up CTA style), first-name-only sign-off, no "following up" opener.

### Email 3, Thread 2 (new thread). Angle: creative angles hiding in the winners. Different substance from Email 1.

**Subject:** your best ad

```text
Sarah, quick guess: your best-performing ad is one your team almost didn't ship, and nobody's fully sure why it works.

That's the pattern at most subscription brands. Creative wins feel random, so the response is to make more of everything and hope the algorithm sorts it out. The catch is that "make more" spreads a small creative team thin, and the real winners get buried under variations of losers.

The brands that break out of that usually aren't making more ads. They've found the two or three angles their actual buyers respond to, and they make those on purpose.

For [Brand], I'd start by pulling the three angles hiding inside your current winners, the ones worth building a real testing lane around.

I can sketch those three from what's already live on your account. Just reply "Yes" and I'll put it together.

Sarah
```

Why it works: a different angle from Email 1 (creative strategy vs audience math), diagnostic-mirror opener with a human-framing token ("quick guess"), one-to-one voice, easy-yes opt-in on a different, concrete deliverable.

### Email 4, Thread 2 (same thread, support). Question CTA. No goodbye.

```text
Sarah, the reason this matters more for subscription than one-off ecom: nail the angle and you're not just lifting first purchase, you're changing who subscribes and stays. The wrong angle sells a discount hunter. The right one sells the person still around at month six.

I can tell which of your live ads are pulling which kind of buyer, just from what's running.

Would it help to see that breakdown for [Brand]?

Sarah
```

Why it works: supports Email 3, adds a new reason (angle changes retention, not just first purchase), closes on a forward-looking question, no last-touch / goodbye framing.

## Register checklist (what to hold when writing in this style)

- [ ] Opens by mirroring the prospect's own situation or their buyers' real pain, not a homepage summary or a generic industry line.
- [ ] Personalization is specific, researched, and true (their positioning, their public numbers, what's live on their account), never a restatement of what they already know.
- [ ] Written as "I" to "you". "We/us" only where it is a genuine team/company fact that can't honestly be said as "I".
- [ ] Length is earned: every line is about THIS prospect. Cut any line a competitor could send, whatever the word count. Long is not the goal; substance is.
- [ ] Email 1 and Email 3 close with "Just reply 'Yes' and I'll [deliverable]." after naming a concrete, deliverable thing.
- [ ] Email 2 and Email 4 close with a forward-looking question, never the opt-in and never a dead end.
- [ ] No "Want me to...", "Want us to send...", "Interested?", "Worth a look?", or any framing that makes the prospect the one wanting / needing / obligated.
- [ ] No em dashes, no spaced em dash, no banned tokens (run `spam-word-checker.md`).
- [ ] Proof points are real and on file (Proof-Asset Gate), or dropped. Placeholders above are not shippable.
- [ ] Signature (full block on Email 1 / Email 3, first name on follow-ups) is set on the sending-tool step, not baked into the body.
