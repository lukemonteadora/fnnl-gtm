## Scope

This is the controlling hard rule for the **first written cold touch** in any outreach channel: cold email, LinkedIn DM, LinkedIn connection note, SMS, X DM, or another written prospecting message.

It does not force the same skeleton onto same-thread follow-ups, warm replies, asset-delivery messages, interested-reply handling, nurture, or call scripts. Those are different jobs and keep their existing channel-specific rules.

## Framework A (house name): the direct concrete offer, Aaron's measured winner anatomy

**What it does.** It leads with a useful, specific free deliverable, shows that the sender handles the work, and asks permission for a small next step. The doctrine names this pattern the direct concrete offer (doctrine §8, the first of his seven frameworks), and it is his default when the buyer understands the benefit without a lesson. Its measured shape is the winner anatomy from his 1.5M-email study (S02, 2026-05-11, doctrine §9). "Framework A" is only our house name for it: his own letter labels are not stable across videos (doctrine §0), so describe it by what it does.

The offer comes before any of this. If the offer is weak, the copy cannot rescue it (doctrine §2, the diagnostic order: buyer, offer, can every yes in the test be honored, and only then the copy).

### The Email 1 contract (B1 to B15)

**B1. Default anatomy: Aaron's measured winner** (the 1.5M-email study, S02 2026-05-11, doctrine §9).

- **Subject:** 2 to 4 words, all lowercase, 0 to 2 variables, curiosity plus relevance. Never deceptive: no "FW:" or fake thread, and no emergency, invoice or account-fault framing (doctrine §9, examples §3).
- **Body:** 25 to 35 words, 2 to 3 sentences, one to two lines, plus an optional PS that is excluded from the count because it stays short and does one job (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21). **No greeting** (doctrine §9: *"you don't even use hey, hi, hello, greetings, or anything."*): "Hey", "Hi" and "Hello" fail, and a bare first name is not a greeting.
- **Opens straight on the offer:** *"no pain, no observation, no preamble"* (doctrine §9, §13).
- **First name optional; it may open the email or sit later in the sentence (his first-name-second trick: *"the first name is not the first thing in the message. It's the second."*, doctrine §9).** His own model emails open on it (examples §1.1, §1.3). (Operator ruling, 2026-09-21: first-name-second is an optional trick, not a rule.)
- **The offer sentence** is usually a conditional around a free concrete deliverable ("if we could [deliverable] at no cost ..."), with the dream outcome implied through the artifact (doctrine §4). Deliverable-first stays the default (his measured winner). A conditional that names the outcome and pairs it with a zero-risk term ("if we could [result]... at no cost", a free trial, zero upfront payment) is also his own shape (doctrine §4; his Jun 29 and Sep 14 scripts) and runs as a labeled arm (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21): no proof claim on line one, no guarantee on the core service, and an outcome that is not a stretch (that last limit is the corpus's reading, not his words). Still banned: an outcome stated with no risk term, and the retired bundle of an outcome plus a named comparable and its number on line one (proof goes to the PS, one point). (This bullet used to add "never around an outcome promise" and "never claimed", a blanket ban that his own Jun 29 and Sep 14 copy contradicts.)
- **Scope disclosure:** three to four real tasks we handle plus the one small thing they do, as a parenthetical or in prose, whenever it reflects the real process. Never invented (doctrine §9: *"three to four things that you actually handle"*).
- **One soft permission CTA.** Default **"Would that be worth sharing more?"**, with variants from examples §4: "Worth sharing more." · "Is that worth sharing more?" · "would that be worth sending over?" · "Mind if I send it to you?". (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21: Aaron's ask replaces "Would that be worth hearing more?" as the first-listed default; the house phrasings stay accepted as variants.)
- **Optional PS, short, doing ONE of four jobs:** (1) the relevance or signal demoted from line one (the opening-line swap, examples §1.1); (2) one same-mechanism near-twin proof point in plain legible numbers (B6); (3) a casual risk-elimination or reason-it's-free line (B7; doctrine §3, supply the reason the thing is free); or (4) the deliverable's turnaround and the prospect's one small act, as in his §1.5 winner: "24-hour turnaround takes you 2 minutes to skim." (examples §1.5).

**B2. Two canonical shapes, A/B-tested** (ledger R14, doctrine §9): the one-liner (offer plus CTA in one sentence, optionally no PS) versus two lines plus a PS. Neither is an error; test them against each other.

**B3. Labeled variants, never the default.** Each one is recorded in `copy.md` as a test arm or as an explicit awareness-driven choice:

- the two-line relevance or pain-first pair (R14): line one is a common validated pain that *"builds a little bit of pain but doesn't focus on it too much"*, and the hook's pain must be the exact pain the offer solves (the hook-payoff rule, doctrine §9);
- an outcome conditional paired with a zero-risk term, his own shape, run as a labeled arm inside the B1 boundary (doctrine §4; his Jun 29 and Sep 14 scripts; operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21);
- a no-CTA variant (examples §4);
- a longer variant for solution-aware or saturated markets (ledger R07, the saturation exception; ledger R19 records how far his Sep 14 client script went), run only as a logged A/B arm;
- a soft neutral question for less-aware buyers (S08, doctrine §8), answerable honestly either way, and never a question-form PAIN opener;
- a brief truthful context line (S13) when the asset needs framing;
- the call-out form, only when the observed thing is what we offer to work on (doctrine §8), and demoted to the PS by default.

R18, his Sep 14 compliment opener, is recorded for study only: he flags it as an exception he is normally against and scopes it to one client's offer. It is not a runnable variant in governed campaigns; running it needs an explicit operator approval as a logged test, with carve-outs to Hard Rules 19, 30 and 34. (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21: this used to sit in the list above as a runnable logged test arm; the Hard Rules named are `/campaign-copywriting`'s.)

**B4. Floor** (doctrine §9: *"It's almost too short… this almost feels like the just bumping it up type follow-up, which everybody hates."*). The floor is about carrying the offer, not a word count: a body a little under 25 words is acceptable whenever it still carries the whole offer (the one-liner is the common case, not the only one). Never so short it reads like a bump.

**B5. Variables.** The first name is optional (B1). **The company-name variable is not used by default** (examples §8: *"which I actually hate"*). (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21) Prefer the case-study variable, used as the **target** of the deliverable (for example, a list of 100 companies that look like [the case study]), never as our proof (doctrine §9). **His variable and the house version differ** (attribution corrected 2026-09-21): his is the prospect's own best client, a per-prospect value (doctrine §3, §5, §9); the house version is a shared near-twin case, named once for the whole slice (B13). **It is a slice-level value, not a per-contact column:** no CSV column exists for it, and under B13 the body is shared per slice, so the slice's case-study name is written into the shared body (or held as a slice-level value). It must be real, sourced, and true for every contact in the slice. The subject carries 0 to 2 variables.

**B6. Proof** (doctrine §9, examples §6). One point maximum. Never in lines one or two: PS only, or deferred to email two. Near-twin and same-mechanism, a plain named number, no acronyms. Never generic ("we've worked with hundreds of clients" is on his refused list). Never Aaron's own totals presented as ours. Never an exact-industry claim for a loosely similar case. A PS cannot contradict the body.

**B7. Risk reversal** (examples §5). Casual risk elimination (his model lines are in the exemplar set below); free until the result; never a money-back guarantee (his verdict: *"incredibly washed out"*). "No strings attached" must be literally true (doctrine §3: remove homework, not honesty).

**B8. Voice** (doctrine §9, Voice). Casual first person, peer-to-peer. Text-from-a-friend but competent: natural is not sloppy, and there is no false intimacy. Plain words, no jargon, never self-declared uniqueness, and it must not read like a template.

**B9. Aaron's kill-list** (doctrine §9 "Banned openers and moves", examples §8). Never:

- *"I hope this email finds you well"* or *"I know you're busy, but…"*;
- introducing yourself or your company;
- fake relevance, or a compliment that would fit anyone;
- asking for a proposal, pricing or a meeting too early (his *"clingy boyfriend"*);
- criticizing the prospect;
- emojis;
- storytelling or picture-painting before the offer;
- **em dashes of any kind in email copy** (*"no m dashes ever, **that screams ChatGPT**"*; this is also the house rule);
- deceptive subject framing, such as an "FW:" fake-forward;
- "audit" as the offer word (doctrine §3; a bounded, evidence-based diagnostic survives under a better name, ledger R09);
- jargon, or self-declared uniqueness;
- a hook-payoff mismatch;
- a question-form pain opener;
- a long essay email;
- a loaded hypothetical ("What if you could double revenue?");
- an unverified problem assumption ("Still losing money to X?" without evidence);
- a free offer that never says "free" or "no cost" (examples §8: *"I think that's a huge miss."*);
- metrics the buyer ignores;
- a robotic, template-shaped message;
- a salesy sign-off ("Thanks for the time");
- opt-out lines or spintax artifacts in the body. The opt-out mechanics a campaign needs (the one-click List-Unsubscribe header, stop-on-reply, suppression of stop replies) live in the sending platform as house compliance rules (B15), and where the law requires a visible notice, B10's legal override applies.

The row-by-row reasons and fixes live in `aaron-examples.md` §8, which is a QA gate (see the end of the exemplar set).

**B10. Signature** (doctrine §9, "Signature and send format"). First name plus company name (first name only is the house reading of his "Just put your name, put your company name"; attribution corrected 2026-09-21). No job title. No location (*"you don't want to bring your location or politics or anything into it."*). Plain text. **Legal requirements override style** (the doctrine's own rule, not Aaron's words: doctrine §11 states "Campaign caps, legal requirements, and opt-outs override." in the corpus author's voice; attribution corrected 2026-09-21): where a client's counsel or the recipient's jurisdiction requires a sender postal address or other identification, add exactly what is required and record it as a compliance requirement, not a style choice. (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21: replaces the three-line full name, title @ company, location block.)

**B11. Editing order** (doctrine §9, load-bearing, do not reorder): 1) fix the offer; 2) remove unsupported facts; 3) make the output and the effort clear; 4) choose one next action; 5) remove filler; 6) only then apply word count, channel rules and style. Then the per-line QA loop: for every line, what job does it do and which fact supports it? Remove lines with no job and flag lines with no evidence. Pre-send test: would I answer this? His version, *"if I received that email, I definitely would not be answering it."*, must be false.

**B12. Portability rule** (doctrine §9): *"it's the **script framework** that's doing all of the heavy lifting here, **not the specific words**."* Aaron's examples show SHAPE. Never paste his sentences into client copy; change the offer, the pain and the CTA wording to the client. The one deliberate exception is the CTA: B1 keeps his own ask as the default wording, because he standardizes the CTA himself (see Skeleton).

**B13. Personalization** (doctrine §8, ledger R02, R13). The signal's primary job is list relevance (*"you can just use that as a way to create a more relevant data source and then the offer then just becomes more potent."*). Personalize the ICP or slice variable; never stack per-contact inserts. **`/campaign-copywriting` Hard Rule 34's fork is resolved in favor of Hard Rule 34** (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21): one shared body per campaign or offer-angle slice. The per-contact `email_N_x` body columns (`email_1_a`, `email_1_b`, `email_2`, optional `email_3`, referenced in the locked sending platform's variable syntax; mechanism unchanged) carry that shared text, varying only the first name. A variable that changes what is delivered, such as the case-study name, is set per slice and written into that slice's shared body, never a per-contact column (B5).

**B14. House hardness, relaxed to Aaron's stated positions** (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21):

- "Exactly one merge token" in the subject becomes **0 to 2 variables** (he says "one to two variables" and shows zero-variable subjects).
- "Exactly one question" becomes **one primary ask**: one CTA question by default, and no stacked asks (doctrine §9: "Give one primary action."). A second question mark appears only in the labeled S08 soft-question variant (B3).
- "Never a question opener" becomes: allowed only as the S08 soft neutral question variant for less-aware buyers (B3). Question-form pain openers stay banned (B9).
- The "mandatory" parenthetical becomes **the default whenever it reflects the real process**, as a parenthetical or in prose, and never fabricated.
- "25 to 35 words, hard cap in every market" becomes **25 to 35 as the default**, with Aaron's floor (B4) and his saturation exception: the longer version runs only as a logged A/B arm (ledger R07).
- The house count included the optional PS in the 25 to 35 words; the count now covers **the body only**, and the PS stays short and does one job (his §1.1 model is about 22 body words plus an 18-word PS).
- "Never around an outcome promise" becomes **the B1 boundary**: deliverable-first stays the default, and an outcome conditional paired with a zero-risk term runs as a labeled arm (B3).

**B15. House safeguards that stay** (this workspace's, never Aaron's; doctrine §13, last paragraph): capability truth; the Proof-Asset Gate; deliverability formatting (plain text, no links or calendar links in emails 1 to 3, and the formatting bans in `/spam-word-checker`); compliance, including the opt-out mechanics in B9; the QA gates; relevance-first subjects (statistics, hype and the offer stay out of the subject, so the offer and its "free" live in the body); plain vocabulary (the house buzzword list); and "a number in the offer". The deliverable's own quantity, turnaround or effort numbers satisfy that last one (the 100 in a 100-company list, a 24-hour turnaround, "takes you 2 minutes"). Any benchmark or cost-of-inaction figure goes to the PS as its single proof or relevance point.

#### §1.1 The opening-line swap `[AS]`, his most transferable move

> **Before (rejected):** "Hey first name, notice you're running static ads on Meta, so thought I'd reach out if we could ideate, script, edit, and record one free UGC ad creative for you. Would that be worth sharing more?"

> **After (his model):** "first name, if we could ideate, script, edit, and record one free UGC ad creative for you. Would that be worth sharing more? PS, thought it'd be relevant since you're currently running ads on Meta and 100% of them are static."

What it shows: same facts, same offer, only the order changes. The offer moves to line one and the relevance is demoted to the PS (B1). The work split rides in prose ("ideate, script, edit, and record"), and there is one free deliverable.

> "first name if we launch your first video in the next 7 days at absolutely no cost."

**Rejected subject lines, with his reasons** `[ANTI]`:
- "free creative ad strategy". His reason: *"someone who knows that they don't really care about ads isn't going to read this at all, even if your offer is fantastic."*
- "Can it be achieved?". His reason: *"they leaned too much into curiosity… you have zero clue what this is going to be about, and because it's so ambiguous, there's nothing that actually makes you want to open."*
- "{first name}, one quick question". His reason: *"they see this, they know it's a cold email… it doesn't allude to what it is that they are potentially interested in at all."*
- An "FW:" fake-forward prefix. His reason: *"almost like a little bit of a trick to make it feel like an internal email… last thing you want to do is start the conversation with deceit."* His honest substitute for the same casual effect is simply lowercase.

**The conditional on using an open role in the subject:** *"I like using the role in the subject line because they might think that you're an applicant… As long as they are the hiring person in that space. If they're not the hiring person, just go back to something a little bit more simple. Maybe don't even mention the role."*

#### §4 CTAs `[AS]`

> **"Would that be worth sharing more?"**

Variants he uses: "Worth sharing more." · "Is that worth sharing more?" · "would that be worth sending over?" · "Mind if I send it to you?" · "Do you mind if I send it to you?" · "If you've got 15 minutes, I can walk you through a few high leverage changes that we'd make for zero cost." · "Open to testing it for free." `[AS-draft]` Jul 13 · "would you be open to trying it out" and then, in the same email, "let me know and I'll shoot you over the trial link" `[AS-client]` Sep 14. He calls the second *"almost like the second CTA"*; both ask for the same reply (doctrine ledger R19).

**What he replaced and why** `[ANTI]`: "would that be worth a chat" / "would that be worth exploring" / "would that be worth whatever." His reason is friction, not phrasing: *"It's very rare that someone is immediately like, yeah, sure, let me just throw you 30 minutes of my time and we'll chat."* The ask is **permission to send something**, not permission to meet.

**He also tests no CTA at all.** His live A/B: *"having two lines plus a PS against something like a one-liner entirely with no PS line, no CTA, all kind of together in one."*

What it shows: the ask is permission to send something, not permission to meet. The B1 default is his signature ask; the no-CTA version runs only as a labeled test arm (B3). His Jul 13 and Sep 14 asks were added to examples §4 on 2026-09-21 and are copied above verbatim. Two cautions for Email 1 (clarified 2026-09-21): his Sep 14 trial-link line is a statement CTA, so it never closes Email 1 on its own (Hard Structure 3 keeps one CTA question as the final main-body sentence, and the Precedence list bars a question-less initial CTA), and his Jul 13 line is punctuated as a statement in the source, so as a close it is asked as a question. The Sep 14 pair asks for the same reply twice (ledger R19: one primary action holds), so that two-CTA shape runs only inside the logged longer arm (B3).

#### §5 Risk reversal: casual risk elimination `[AS]`

He retired the guarantee himself and named the replacement. *"What really works in 2026 is basically just like casual risk elimination. Something that sounds like a reasonable person just being up front."*

> "We'll do this for free and then we only move forward if we actually see something worth implementing."
> "If it's not a fit, no hard feelings. I just want to get in front of you."

**Why pay-on-result beats plain free:** *"You're actually doing it for free until you get the result and then that's when you get paid. Which again, makes people's BS radar just go off a little bit less."*

> "This isn't necessarily too good to be true because it's actually a mechanism that we use to find some of our best clients."

**Rejected** `[ANTI]`: the money-back guarantee, *"in my opinion, incredibly washed out."* And "get results in 90 days or you don't pay", because *"there is no definition of results at all."*

What it shows: B7. Casual, reasonable-person risk elimination, free until the result, never a money-back guarantee. Any "no strings attached" must be literally true (doctrine §3).

#### §6 The PS proof template `[AS]`

> "Client went from X bad result to X good result using this exact process."

**Rules that travel with it:** one proof point maximum (*"if you have more than one proof point in your email, you're over justifying"*), never in line one or two, PS only, and often deferred entirely to email two: *"these PS lines not necessary… In some cases, we actually see social proof in certain areas almost just like make the message a little bit salesier. So often times we'll save it for email two."*

**Proof phrasing he refuses** `[ANTI]`: "we've worked with hundreds of clients" · "here's what our clients have to say" · "We helped so-and-so brand go from 1 mil to 10 mil in revenue in 3 years, blah blah blah blah." His verdict on the category: *"generic social proof is completely useless and I almost avoid using it most cases."*

**The acronym rule.** `[ANTI]` "one swimwear brand dropped CPAC by 76%" fails because *"as soon as you jump into really crazy acronyms and things like that, you lost them. Like people want to know that you made more money, you saved more money… I run Meta ads and I don't even know what that means."* His fix is a plain, named, legible number: "Nani Swimwear saw a 942% lift in ads to cart."

What it shows: B6. One near-twin, same-mechanism point in plain legible numbers, PS only or email two. Never Aaron's own figures as ours, and never an exact-industry claim for a loosely similar case.

Every draft, before it is shown, is checked against Aaron's rejection bank, `aaron-examples.md` §8 (what he killed on camera, his verbatim reason, his fix), and his anti-pattern offers in §9, both in `/ask-aaron`. His own one-line version of the whole bank is the pre-send test in B11. Cite rows by section; do not copy the bank into this file.

## Hard Structure

Every initial cold message must pass all of these checks. Each one names the contract rule it enforces.

1. **Open with the offer (B1).** No greeting word ("Hey", "Hi", "Hello" fail). A first name is optional; it may open the email or sit later in the sentence (his first-name-second trick). The first substantive words state the specific free deliverable (in the labeled outcome-conditional arm, the outcome paired with its zero-risk term, B1). No observation, pain line, question, industry setup, compliment, sender introduction, or preamble comes first, except inside a labeled B3 variant.
2. **Length (B1, B4, B14).** 25 to 35 body words is the default. Count the body only: the PS is excluded from the count (it stays short and does one job), and so are the subject and the signature. Count whitespace-delimited tokens, so a hyphenated term with no spaces counts as one word (a house convention). Under the B4 floor, a body a little under 25 words is acceptable whenever it still carries the whole offer (the one-liner is the common case, not the only one) and cannot read like a bump; his §1.1 model body is about 22 words. Over 35 runs only as a logged A/B arm for a solution-aware or saturated market (R07). (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21: the house used to count the PS inside the 25 to 35 words.)
3. **Sentences and the ask (B1, B14).** 2 to 3 main-body sentences, or one for the B2 one-liner. The optional PS sits outside the sentence count too, and is declarative. One primary ask: one CTA question by default, as the final main-body sentence, with no stacked asks. A second question mark is allowed only in the labeled S08 soft-question variant, and zero only in the labeled no-CTA arm (B3). (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21)
4. **Scope disclosure when it is real (B1, B14).** Name the 3 to 4 things the sender genuinely handles and the one small action the prospect takes, in parentheses or in prose. When the real process does not support it (for example, the prospect's only act is a one-word yes and the PS carries the effort, as in examples §1.5), leave it out rather than invent tasks. Inside parentheses use a semicolon, comma, or period, never a dash. (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21)
5. **End with soft permission (B1).** Default `Would that be worth sharing more?`, or a question-form variant from examples §4 (his statement-form asks there, such as the Sep 14 trial-link line, do not close Email 1 on their own; see the §4 exemplar note). House variants of the same permission-to-send ask stay accepted: `Would that be worth hearing more?`, `Open to it?`, `May I send it over?`, `Can I send it across?`, `Would it help if I sent it?`. The CTA must follow a named deliverable.
6. **Say "free" (B9).** When the deliverable is free, the copy says "free" or "no cost".
7. **Proof only when it is true, compact, and in the PS (B6).**
8. **Keep the offer real (B15, house).** The free deliverable, sender workload, proof, and prospect action must all be accurate, and every yes must have a named honorer for this arm's test flight (the agency by default, the client only with `lead_magnet_client_ok: yes`); full-volume capacity is asked after the test in `/offer-test-slate`, never here.

## Channel Adaptation

- **Cold email:** a 2-to-4-word lowercase subject with 0 to 2 variables and curiosity plus relevance (B1), in the shape of his "question about topic" (examples §3). Keep statistics, hype, and the offer out of the subject (house, B15). (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21: was "exactly one merge token, such as `fyi {{first_name}}`".)
- **LinkedIn DM, SMS, X DM, and other subjectless channels:** omit the subject and use the same body contract.
- **Connection-note or character-capped channel:** use the B2 one-liner (the offer plus the permission ask). Drop the optional PS first, then the scope disclosure, before touching the offer. If the offer and the ask cannot fit truthfully, stop instead of deleting a required element.

## Skeleton

Placeholders are in square brackets. No greeting and no company-name variable (B1, B5). The skeleton shows structure, not wording (B12), with one deliberate exception: the CTA wording is fixed. Aaron standardizes the CTA himself (doctrine §9, step 9 of his on-camera writing process: he standardizes the CTA across all of his variations to one line), and his signature ask recurs across his own examples (examples §4), so B1 makes it the default; the examples §4 variants and the house variants stay accepted (clarified 2026-09-21).

```text
Subject (email only): [2 to 4 lowercase words, 0 to 2 variables, curiosity plus relevance]

If we could [specific free deliverable, aimed at the slice's case study when the offer uses one] at no cost ([task 1], [task 2], and [task 3] on our side; you'd only [one small action]), would that be worth sharing more?

PS, [optional, one job only: the relevance line demoted from line one, OR one same-mechanism near-twin proof point in plain numbers, OR a casual risk-elimination or reason-it's-free line, OR the deliverable's turnaround and the prospect's one small act]
```

Without the PS this is the B2 one-liner. For the other canonical shape (two lines plus a PS), put the offer on line one and the ask on line two, as in examples §1.1. The first name is optional; it may open the email or sit later in the sentence (his first-name-second trick). A case-study name, when the offer uses one, is the slice's shared near-twin case (the house version of his per-prospect variable, B5) and is written into this shared body.

## Failure Gate

Framework A has no setup paragraph to rescue a weak offer. If the message needs an explanation before the offer, the offer is not ready. Aaron: *"if the offer is weak, vague, or unaligned with the prospect's worldview, then there's nothing in the email to save it."* (doctrine §9)

Do not rescue a weak offer by switching the initial cold touch to a pain-led or observation-led opening. The B3 variants are test arms and awareness choices, never a repair. Walk the diagnostic instead (doctrine §2), and fix one of these:

- replace a vague offer or a service description with a named deliverable
- make the output and the prospect's effort clear (the real scope disclosure, or a PS that carries the effort)
- narrow or change the audience so the offer is immediately relevant
- replace a free offer nobody can honor during the test with one the agency can honor, or get the client's OK for it (`/offer-test-slate`)

Do not produce sendable copy until the failure is fixed or the operator explicitly overrides this hard rule.

## Precedence

This contract overrides older outreach-copy instructions that require or allow any of the following for the initial cold touch:

- 50-to-90-word or 70-to-90-word first messages as the default (a longer version runs only as a logged A/B arm, R07)
- a signal, observation, pain line, question, proof line, or setup paragraph before the offer, outside a labeled B3 variant
- an initial CTA with no question, including `Just reply "Yes"...`, outside a labeled no-CTA test arm (B3)
- a greeting word ("Hey", "Hi", "Hello") or the company-name variable by default (B1, B5)
- more than one primary ask
- an outcome promise outside the B1 boundary (an outcome stated with no risk term, or the retired bundle of an outcome plus a named comparable and its number on line one) or any proof claim in line one (B1, B6); an outcome conditional paired with a zero-risk term runs only as the labeled arm (B1, B3) (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21)
- per-contact inserts stacked into the body (B13)

Deliverability, truth, capability, proof, compliance, and fulfilment gates still apply. When one of those gates conflicts with squeezing more material into the default length, keep the safety gate and remove non-required copy. Never invent or overstate a claim to satisfy the framework.

## Mechanical QA

Before showing, saving, serializing, or uploading the first cold message, record:

- channel
- shape: the B2 one-liner, two lines plus a PS, or the name of a labeled B3 variant and its test-arm id
- body word count, PS excluded from the count (it stays short and does one job): 25 to 35 by default; a little under 25 whenever the body still carries the whole offer (B4, not only for the one-liner); over 35 only with its logged A/B arm id (R07) (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21)
- main-body sentence count, excluding the optional PS: 2 to 3, or 1 for the one-liner
- primary asks: exactly 1; question marks: 1 by default (2 only in the labeled S08 variant, 0 only in the labeled no-CTA arm)
- greeting word: none ("Hey", "Hi", "Hello" fail); the first name is optional and may open the email or sit later in the sentence
- first substantive content: the specific free deliverable (in the labeled outcome-conditional arm, the outcome paired with its zero-risk term, B1)
- "free" or "no cost": stated whenever the deliverable is free
- scope disclosure: present with 3 to 4 real sender tasks and exactly 1 small prospect action, or recorded as not applicable with the reason
- soft permission CTA: the final main-body sentence, in the default wording or a question-form examples §4 or house variant
- PS: absent, or short, declarative, and doing one of the four B1 jobs (demoted relevance, one near-twin proof point, risk elimination or the reason it is free, or the deliverable's turnaround and the prospect's one small act)
- proof: none, or one near-twin point in the PS, verified on file
- variables: no company-name variable by default; a case-study name only as the slice's own value, written into the shared body and sourced, never a per-contact column
- email subject, when applicable: 2 to 4 words, lowercase, 0 to 2 variables, no deceptive framing
- em dashes: none of any kind
- rejection bank (`aaron-examples.md` §8) and kill-list (B9): checked
- pre-send test (B11): "would I answer this?" answered yes
- proof: verified; fulfilment: every yes has a named honorer

Any failed row blocks the copy.
