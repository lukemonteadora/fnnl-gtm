## Core Philosophy

Every campaign has two levers:
1. **The List** - Who we're reaching out to (broad -> niche)
2. **The Message** - What value proposition we're leading with

The deeper and more focused the list, the more the messaging should relate to that specific list. Broad lists require AI-generated personalization to feel relevant. Niche lists can reference the filtering criteria directly.

**The sharpest form of "the list is the message" is who the company sells to.** Segmenting on what a company *is* (industry, size, tech stack) rarely changes the message; segmenting on **who it sells to** (the `sold_to` axis: their customers' vertical, size, and the department they sell into) almost always does, because their customers set their buyer, their pain, and the proof that lands. So a data point that would change what you'd say is the reason to give its slice its own message; one that would not stays a targeting filter under a shared message. This is encoded below as the **Message-differential split test** (the ≥500-company floor governs launch eligibility; the test governs which slices get their own divergent copy) and the sold-to slices in the Signal-Driven Slice Library.

List quality starts with source choice. Do not assume a general-purpose contact database, LinkedIn, or Google Maps is the right source just because it is easy. If a directory, event page, job board, partner marketplace, social signal, public website pattern, or custom scrape proves the campaign better, name that source path.

When source options are unclear, read `/lead-list-tech-stack-research` and separate source roles by layer: the workflow layer (the list workbench in `run_state.list_workbench` by default, with rows pushed in by webhook and zero workbench credits; Claude Code native as the fallback when no workbench table is ready), source-of-truth signal, collection tool, contact enrichment, and QA. The layer order is the reasoning; which tool fills each layer comes from the contract, never from habit. A contact database is usually the contact-enrichment layer, not the source-of-truth signal.

Every campaign idea must pass a source reality check for the target location. If the target market is Denmark, do not lazily write "scrape directories" unless you know which Denmark-relevant directories, registries, associations, job boards, marketplaces, event pages, or public databases could work. If a global contact database is the best source, say why. If it is weak in that market, say that too.

### Value Proposition Categories

Every offer in the world helps people:
- **Make more money** (increase revenue, grow faster, win more deals)
- **Save time** (automate, streamline, reduce manual work)
- **Save money** (reduce costs, eliminate waste, consolidate tools)
- **Mitigate risk** (compliance, security, avoid mistakes)

### Targeting Levels

- **Broad**: The widest possible audience within the given constraints. Requires strong AI personalization to feel relevant.
- **Focused**: One additional filter on top of broad (e.g., new hires, 10+ years in business, specific technology usage).
- **Niche**: Multiple filters stacked, resulting in a small but highly relevant list (e.g., LinkedIn engagers + new hire + specific industry + specific tech stack).

### Launch waves: the whole TAM at full volume, with the offer test inside it

The whole reason the TAM is built first is so the launch set is decided from **the signals companies actually carry**, not from guesswork. **Sequencing still matters, but it sequences what is tested, not how much is sent (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes the "first wave is a small, focused set, not to scale" reading of the offer-validation-first HARD RULE, 2026-07-04).** For a new client whose offer is not yet market-proven, the first wave is **the whole qualified TAM at full volume**, and the offer test runs inside that full send: the offer is still the first variable tested (the biggest lever, per the four-lever order offer → list → personalization → volume), but Aaron validates the offer *by* sending volume, not before it (Sep 21: the whole mapped TAM of one vertical, 19,600 leads, about 96,000 sends in one week). What waits for a proven offer is the signal-slice fan-out, never the rest of the TAM. The wave is shaped **Volume-first** (the HARD RULE below): it covers the qualified TAM through **a few big campaigns** that carry the bulk of the sends, plus, once the offer is proven, a bounded testing allocation for new signal slices; for a client whose offer is already proven (`run_state.offer_market_proven = true`) that coverage wave is the first wave. What the wave maximizes is **covered volume and reach**, never the number of campaigns, so this is not a "launch a campaign per signal" posture and equally not a "start with a token 2 to 4 and see" one. The limits on the wave are the **≥500-company eligibility floor** (below), **message-differentiation** (which slices earn their own copy), the **test budget** (for signal slices), and **sending capacity**, which is requested here and sized to the wave, and which stretches the runway when the fleet delivers less, never cutting offers or TAM rows (the capacity request, below).

**HIGH-VOLUME DEFAULT (operator directive 2026-07-07, raises the numbers below).** The default posture is now explicitly high volume: **2,000 to 30,000 emails SENT per day per client** across a few big campaigns, and **1,000 up to 20,000 leads per campaign** (not the old 1,000 to 10,000). Three things follow, and they bind the sizing below:
> - **Capacity is the binding constraint, and it is sized to the wave, never used to trim it (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes setting the real daily number at launch by whatever warmed fleet happens to exist and ratcheting it up from the low end).** The daily-send number a client can actually hit = `active warmed mailboxes × safe-per-mailbox-rate` (rate ~15 to 20 cold sends/mailbox/day, and only one rotation-half sends at a time). So propose the high-volume shape and write the **capacity request** (below), sized to first-touch the whole TAM in about one Monday-to-Friday sending week; `/mailbox-mix-strategy` sizes the client's fleet to it (or `/mailbox-pool-staging` assigns a pre-warmed pool group), and `/cold-email-kickoff` surfaces the setup total for the operator's approval plus the warmup runway. Never promise a daily volume the fleet can't safely send: until the fleet is live and warmed, the real daily number is what it delivers, and a smaller or still-warming fleet stretches the runway rather than cutting offers or TAM rows. Ground truth is what the client's warmed fleet actually sends: read it before committing to a number.
> - **Per-campaign daily throughput scales; campaign COUNT stays small.** A big campaign's `max_new_leads/day` is raised in proportion to its assigned warmed mailbox pool (`campaign daily sends ≈ mailboxes assigned × rate`), NOT held at the 500 default. You do NOT reach 30k/day by launching 60 tiny campaigns; you feed a handful of big campaigns a large fleet each.
> - **Volume per campaign = `enriched companies × contacts-per-company × email-yield (~50 to 70%)`.** Compute and record this forecast for every campaign and check it against the 1,000 to 20,000 band. The biggest lever is **contacts-per-company** (pull **every** ICP-matching decision-maker on every company, per the scrape-all rule in the ≥500-company floor below, never a 1-2 or 2-to-4 cap; `/tam-build` Layer 1d), which turns a company universe that saturates at a few thousand companies into a 10k+ lead campaign. The company universe is widened by the expansion-for-volume ladder in `/tam-build` (on the default filter route: more keyword variants → markets → headcount bands → merge sibling segments → the widening top-up; on a lookalike-route group, seeds and similarity come first), each step gated on ICP fit ("when it makes sense only").

**Volume-first, not campaign-count-first (the GOVERNING default, Eric, HARD RULE 2026-07-04, numbers raised by the high-volume default above). It governs the shape of every wave in this skill; the "maximize campaigns" framing it re-scoped used to run above and below it and has now been removed from this file (encoded 2026-07-17).** Cold email is a volume game, and the volume lives in a FEW BIG campaigns, not many small ones. So the coverage-wave objective is to maximize covered **volume and reach**, NOT the raw **count** of campaigns. Concretely, on any TAM big enough to support it (the Volume-adequacy check below):
- **The bulk of every wave's sends goes into a small number of LARGE campaigns:** the broad value-prop over the whole qualified TAM, plus the big message-differential cuts (the `sold_to` segments, which are large buckets). These are the volume campaigns and should run into the **thousands to tens of thousands of leads where the TAM allows** (a live broad slice on a prior client build carried ~1,500; the high-volume target is 1,000 to 20,000 per campaign, reached via buying-committee depth + the `/tam-build` expansion-for-volume ladder). This is where "expect 1,000 to 20,000 leads per campaign" is true.
- **New or unproven SIGNAL slices are a bounded TESTING allocation (~5 to 10% of wave volume, Eric's ratio), not the default shape of the wave, and they run only once the offer is proven (never on a dead or still-unread offer).** Launch a handful of the highest-confidence signal experiments through `/experiment-design` to discover lift, then **scale the winners back into the big campaigns** rather than leaving volume fragmented across dozens of 50-to-200-contact slices. A signal that neither changes the message nor is a live experiment **rides the broad/sold-to campaign as a tagged attribution bucket**, not its own small campaign.
- **So "launch every viable signal slice" is re-scoped:** the ≥500-company floor is *eligibility*, never a mandate to launch that slice standalone, and campaign COUNT is minimized to what volume, message-differentiation, and the test budget actually require. The one exception is a genuinely small niche whose whole TAM is small (a narrow regional vertical, say): there, small campaigns are correct and the ICP-adequacy check fires instead. The honest boundary: a big TAM belongs in a few big campaigns; a small niche stays small; never fragment a big TAM into a pile of tiny slices to inflate the campaign count.

**Offer-Validation Wave = the whole qualified TAM at full volume, with the offer test running inside it (HARD RULE, new client or any offer not yet market-proven; Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes the "small, focused set" first wave and "size the validation list to the offers, 3 to 4 offers need about 8,000 to 12,000 contacts", operator decision 2026-09-22).** The offer is locked from *research* (`/offer-intelligence` + `/lead-magnet-brainstorm`), which is not the same as *market-proven*. Slicing ten signal micro-campaigns on an unvalidated offer risks ten flops that burn freshly-warmed capacity and first-touch reputation to learn one thing (the offer is weak) that the offer read would have told you first. So when `run_state.offer_market_proven` is false (the default for a new client), the **first wave is an Offer-Validation Wave that sends the whole qualified TAM**, differentiated by **message** rather than by a sparse timing signal, with several offers running as equal cells inside it. Aaron sizes the offers to the list, never the list to the offers.
> - **The whole qualified TAM goes out.** Every `send_eligible` contact, P1 to P5 in score order, across the wave's first-wave segments, which are non-overlapping and deduped like any wave: the **tight core** (the first filter pull by default, or the seeded core on a lookalike-route group, per `/tam-build`'s Filter-First Discovery HARD RULE, operator decision 2026-09-28; the offer's cleanest best-fit read), the **widened expansion rows** from the `/tam-build` expansion-for-volume ladder (the broad value-prop read, Section 2's No-AI / broad pattern, minimal personalization), the **sold-to cuts** (the `sold_to` axis: message-differentiated by who the company sells to, which reshapes the buyer, pain and proof **without** needing a timing signal, so the wave is differentiated but not thin), and any **data-source split** (below). No company and no segment is held back for a later coverage wave (the per-company contact stagger above still applies: rank 3+ contacts in a company stay queued for a later stagger unless the email step promotes one to replace an exhausted higher-priority contact). When the client's fleet delivers less than the wave needs, the runway stretches and nothing is cut (the capacity request, below).
> - **The offer count follows the list (Aaron, Apr 6: about one front-end offer per 2,000 leads; May 4: always test three or four).** First-wave offer count = `max(3, floor(qualified_contacts / 2000))`, where `qualified_contacts` is the wave's send-eligible contacts, capped at the number of qualifying candidates (fewer than 3 only when fewer qualify, and the report says so). The qualifying candidates are the primary `offer.lead_magnet` plus the `offer.validation_arms` that can be built today, `pending` arms included and `no` or `vetoed` arms excluded, in rank order; a single-offer wave validates one guess. The list is never shrunk to fit a chosen number of offers. Every arm inside the count launches in the first flight (`validation_arms[].flight = 1`); **only arms beyond the count queue** for a later flight (`flight = 2`) (supersedes "the primary plus at least arm 1; further arms join only when warmed send capacity gives each its floor inside the ~3 to 4 week read window", operator directive 2026-09-22). **An existing offer that `/offer-intelligence` validation mode rated Go** needs no new arms: its first wave tests segments and data sources plus positioning variants of that offer (Aaron, Apr 13), and the report says so.
> - **Offers are placed by fit, not run everywhere, as equal cells on shared ground launched together (operator decision 2026-09-23; Aaron Sep 7: each offer matched to the tier it fits, "this is going to be the one where the GLP-1 offer works the best"; replaces "every offer across every segment", 2026-09-22, which cited Aaron Apr 6 "run the offers across all of your campaigns" and Apr 13 "read it matrix style").** The count rule above is unchanged; what changed is where each offer runs (below, "arm" means each offer in the wave, the primary included). An offer's cell is every first-wave contact that passes **that offer's own** fit filters (its `offer_fit_disqualifiers`, any arm-only disqualifiers, and its `offer.icp_inclusion`), so an offer runs in every first-wave segment it fits and in none it does not. Where two or more offers fit the same rows, those rows are split evenly, at random and disjointly across the fitting offers (equal cells on shared ground). Each offer is read **pooled** across every segment it runs in, against the ~1,300 floor as before (the flip criterion below). The winner pick compares offers only on ground they share (matched counts in the segments where both run); an offer that runs alone in a segment is judged there only against the absolute PROVEN and DEAD lines. Placement comes from each offer's own filters applied at cell assignment (Shared disqualifiers, below), never from a desk guess. Inside the wave, P1 to P5 score order still sends the best-fit rows first (Aaron Sep 7: "run A and B first"). For a client whose offers all fit the whole list, nothing changes: every offer runs in every segment on equal cells. Per-buyer-type offer sets (`offer.segments`) are unchanged: each buyer type keeps its own offer set, and fit-matched placement applies across the list segments inside it (Per buyer type, below). > **The flip criterion (pooled within each arm, with an explicit hold state). HARD RULE, re-derived 2026-07-17; replaces the old per-campaign ~300-send version.** The old rule held each campaign to ~300 first-touch sends, then flipped `offer_market_proven` true at roughly 1 positive per 400 contacted or dead at worse than 1 per 1,200. It could not actually decide. A positive reply at the 1-per-400 band is a rare event (Poisson mean 0.75 across 300 sends), so a genuinely good offer shows **zero** positives about 47% of the time and a dead offer shows at least one about 22% of the time: a coin toss dressed as a gate. That is the real reason `offer_market_proven` has never flipped for any client (that, plus most validation waves simply have not sent yet). Two corrections, both encoded here.
>
> **1. Judge each arm POOLED within itself, never across arms (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21).** Each arm is one offer, and the flag gates one decision about it (scale it to the rest of the TAM and open signal slices on it, or iterate it), so pool that arm's first-touch contacts into a single count `n` and its positive replies into a single count `k`. Every arm's pool is its cells across every first-wave segment it runs in (fit-matched placement, above), the primary's included. Never add one arm's contacts or replies to another's. Count a positive from **any** step of the sequence (not first-touch only). This also fixes an old denominator bug: the previous rule read a full-sequence reply against a first-touch-only send count. **The read window: floor first, then sequence plus grace.** An arm (or an in-campaign copy variant) is read once it has reached its ~1,300 first-touch floor AND its last contacts have had the 2-email sequence plus a few days of grace (about 7 to 10 days from their first touch). Never read early because a window passed (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes the fixed ~21-day window). Read daily for the first week after launch. When the runway stretches, the floor, not the window, is usually the binding limit. At each arm read, `/cold-email-weekly-rhythm` also runs `/market-evidence feedback`, which turns the arm's replies into evidence rows; research updates never move the flip. **Guard against a pooled false positive:** for an arm that runs in two or more segments, no single segment cell may carry its flip on its own, and if it clears the bar only on a best-fit segment (say the tight core) while its widened and broad cells stay flat, that is a **list** finding (the offer lands on a narrower list), recorded in the arm's per-segment report, not an offer win, so do not flip `proven` on it. An arm whose own fit filters place it in one segment only is read on that segment (operator decision 2026-09-23).
>
> **2. Three states, not two: PROVEN / DEAD / HOLD.** With `n` = the arm's pooled first-touch contacts and `k` = its pooled positive replies:
> - **Flip PROVEN** (`run_state.offer_market_proven = true` once the arm is picked as the winner below, which then takes the rest of the TAM) when `k >= 2 + n/660`. A genuinely strong offer (1-per-400) only reaches this line on average at about 2,000 pooled contacts: at 1,300 to 2,000 it flips PROVEN roughly 35 to 40% of the time, and at 3,000 about 60% (Poisson odds on this formula). The offer-count formula gives each arm about 2,000 or more first touches whenever the qualified TAM is 6,000+ contacts and the offers fit the whole list (an offer placed on narrower ground gets fewer), where a strong offer's read becomes likely; read HOLD below that as a reading in progress, not a failure.
> - **Flip DEAD** (deactivate the arm; if every arm is dead, iterate the **offer** via `/offer-intelligence` validation mode: Go / Iterate / Swap, plus Eric's SQL to MQL and chunk-up/down reframes; never fan out signal slices on a dead offer) when `k <= n/660 - 2`. This line is unreachable below ~1,300 pooled contacts, so a zero-positive arm under ~1,300 is **not** a kill. You may not call an arm dead below ~1,300 pooled first-touch contacts.
> - **HOLD** in between: keep sending the arm through its cells and let its pooled sample grow, but do not fan out signal slices on it and do not tear up the offer. Hold is active, not idle. The arm keeps sending, so `n` climbs on its own toward one of the two boundaries, until a winner is picked, when the winner-pick retirement (below) parks it.
>
> Ready-reckoner (the same formula, rounded, positives needed to flip): at ~1,300 pooled contacts, PROVEN at k>=4 or DEAD at k=0; ~2,000 pooled, PROVEN at k>=6 or DEAD at k<=1; ~3,000 pooled, PROVEN at k>=7 or DEAD at k<=2; ~4,000 pooled, PROVEN at k>=9 or DEAD at k<=4. Any k between the two numbers at a given size is HOLD.

> **Picking the winner among arms (operator-approved Aaron alignment, 2026-09-21; doctrine §2 step 5, §4).** The flip above decides which arms are PROVEN; the meetings metric decides which PROVEN arm scales, which is where Aaron applies it. Report **time-agreed replies per 1,000 first touches** beside the positive-reply rate for every arm and campaign (a time-agreed reply is a positive reply whose thread moves toward a time, counted with the magnet-inflation guard's sending-platform proxy below). Among the arms that reach PROVEN, the winner that becomes, or stays, `offer.lead_magnet` is the one that does not lose on time-agreed replies per 1,000; compare arms only on the ground they share (matched counts in the segments where both run), where the list and the copy skeleton are identical, and judge an offer that runs alone in a segment there only against the absolute PROVEN and DEAD lines (fit-matched placement, operator decision 2026-09-23). **Pick only when the comparison is fair (operator directive 2026-09-22):** an arm with more first touches reaches PROVEN sooner on volume alone. Equal cells on shared ground make the counts match there by design, but the runway can still reach one arm's cells first, so pick the winner only once every running arm is PROVEN or DEAD, or has at least as many first touches as the leading arm, and judge the pick on matched cell counts on shared ground, never one arm's larger total against another's smaller one. **Winner-pick retirement (house rule).** At the winner pick (made on matched cell counts on shared ground), arms still in HOLD stop receiving new first touches, their live sequences finish, the untouched rows the winner fits go to it (rows it does not fit follow the reallocation-by-fit rule in the first-wave bullets above), and the parked arm's read is recorded as unresolved so it can return as a later test (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes 'another arm's win never retires it'). The untouched rows in any DEAD arm's cells go the same way (to the winner where it fits them, otherwise by fit). Losers are switched off by the DEAD line and by the winner pick, never by a ratio: at ~1,300 contacts an arm exactly as good as the leader shows 2 or fewer positives about 37% of the time (Poisson, mean 3.25), so a relative cut such as "under half the leader's rate" would kill good offers (considered and rejected 2026-09-22; Aaron gives no threshold, Mar 9). Record each arm's read in `offer.validation_arms[].read` (`n`, `k`, time-agreed replies per 1,000, verdict, date) and set its status (`works` for a PROVEN arm that did not win; `hold` for one still reading, which at the pick means parked: no new first touches, live sequences finish, read verdict recorded as `unresolved`). A PROVEN arm whose time-agreed replies per 1,000 are zero or clearly lag the other arms is labeled **farming interest** (doctrine §4: replies from people who were never going to buy): it is not scaled, and the action is to reshape the offer so it filters better by making it more specific (*"specific enough to filter out those who don't and compelling enough to pull in those that do"*), not to kill it. "Clearly lag" has no numeric cut-off: it is judged against the other arms, and the operator confirms the label. The flag and the coverage wave follow the winner, so if every PROVEN arm carries that label there is no winner yet: reshape and read again rather than fanning out. `/lead-magnet-brainstorm` Save Output records a promotion.

> **Two floors, do not merge them.** The ~300-first-touch-sends figure (`/cold-email-weekly-rhythm`) is the per-campaign **raw-reply** readability floor and stays correct for that job (raw reply runs ~1 to 3%, so 300 sends gives a readable raw count, and it gates a negative call inside a >=3-mature-campaign check). This offer flip reads the far rarer **positive**-reply event, so it is judged **pooled per arm at the ~1,300+ floor above**, never per-campaign at 300. It also needs the **positive** reply count specifically, so if reply categorization is off, hand-read and tag the replies first (raw reply rate cannot flip this flag). Tag the time-agreed replies as well, for the winner pick above.

> **The magnet-inflation guard on the flip (HARD RULE, 2026-07-17).** The flip criterion is measuring **the offer**, and it only measures the offer if the offer is what earned the reply. A strong free asset earns yeses on its own merits (the asset is interesting, so people say send it), and those yeses say nothing about whether anyone wants the paid thing behind it. Aaron Shepherd's own warning is the source: his 589 positive replies came from a lead-magnet front end, and he states plainly that getting a reply and booking a call are two completely different things, and that magnet-led campaigns routinely produce a flood of yeses with a terrible meeting-to-lead ratio. A magnet-inflated positive-reply rate that flips `offer_market_proven = true` scales an offer nobody has actually validated across the rest of the TAM and into signal slices. That is the exact failure this whole gate exists to prevent, arriving through the front door with good-looking numbers.

If the wave's E1 is instead **asset-led** (an easy-yes opt-in fronting a real magnet), the reply rate is a read on the *asset*, not the offer, and **it cannot flip the flag by itself**. In that case require a downstream signal that the offer (not the asset) is what's working: meetings booked, or positive replies that survive the asset hand-off and convert to a booked call. Judge on the meeting rate (`meeting_book_rate`, defined in `outbound/reference/reply-scoring-core.md`), not the yes rate. If neither is measurable yet, hold the flag at false and keep reading; a false `proven` is far more expensive than a slow one. (An archetype K interactive tool, or a per-account custom artifact, runs as its own arm and is read the same way: its positive replies alone never flip the flag, because these are the strongest reply-inflators we have. **Message-differential split test: what earns a slice its OWN copy (Eric "the list is the message" doctrine, 2026-07-04).** The ≥500-company floor above is *launch eligibility*: clearing it makes a slice eligible, and how many slices become their own standalone campaign is governed by Volume-first above (most ride the big broad/sold-to campaigns; standalone signal campaigns are the bounded test allocation). Layered on top is a separate *copy-treatment* question: does this data point change **what you'd say**? A launched slice earns its **own divergent copy/angle** only when the data point changes the message. A slice that clears ≥500 but wouldn't change the copy (a `headcount_range` band, `founded_year` on its own, a title like CEO-vs-founder) still launches, but as a **targeting filter / attribution bucket** riding the wave's shared copy, not with its own written angle. Segmenting on **who the company sells to** (the `sold_to` axis: their customers' vertical, size, and the department they sell into) **almost always** changes the message (different buyer, different pain, different proof) and is the strongest reason to write a slice its own copy. So the two decisions stay distinct: **a ≥500-company slice is launch-eligible** (Volume-first governs standalone-vs-tagged), and **write divergent copy only for the message-differential ones** (never manufacture a different message for a slice whose data point doesn't change what you'd say, the copy-side twin of "never contrive a thin slice to inflate the count" below). This is the same targeting-filter-vs-copy-variable line the department-headcount caveat already draws, generalized: a data point that only *selects* the list is a filter; one that changes the *message* earns its own copy.

Launch eligibility (the ≥500-company floor) and copy divergence (message-differential) are two different decisions; both hold, and neither reduces the other.

- **Wave 1: coverage wave.** *For an already-proven offer (`run_state.offer_market_proven = true`) it is the first wave: the whole qualified TAM through the big campaigns. For a new client the Offer-Validation Wave has already sent the whole TAM and the winner took every untouched row it fits at the pick (rows it does not fit went to the strongest offer that fits them), so the coverage wave adds what the offer test deliberately held back, the signal slices, on the proven winner (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes "runs only after the Offer-Validation Wave passes" as a volume gate).* Shape it **Volume-first**: the bulk of the wave's sends goes into a **few big campaigns** (the broad value-prop over the whole qualified TAM, plus the big sold-to message-differential cuts), and the eligible signal slices ride those campaigns as tagged attribution buckets, except for the bounded ~5-10% testing allocation that runs as standalone experiments. Every campaign in the wave must (1) clear the **≥500-company eligibility floor** and (2) not overlap, so dedup the selected slices against each other and the client suppression registry so no company is contacted by two campaigns at once. Hold the **offer and copy approach constant on the signal-test slices**, where attribution is the point; vary only the slice/signal (and its list-appropriate personalization). Holding offer+copy constant there is what keeps the test clean: every reply-rate difference traces to the list, so it is an *attributable* baseline (what `/experiment-design` needs), not a confounded test. The big campaigns carry the winner's positioning variants instead (Wave 2). What is maximized here is covered **volume and reach**, never the campaign count, and it comes only from real, non-overlapping, viable slices, never from contriving thin slices to inflate the number.
- **Wave 2: positioning of the winner, then CTA and delivery, then subject lines (starts at the winner pick, inside the big campaigns; Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes holding positioning tests until the coverage wave has built a baseline).** As soon as the winner is picked, positioning variants of it (the value prop or pain point it leads with, the mechanism, and Creative Ideas, the three-ideas positioning of the offer) start inside the big campaigns as in-campaign variants, one variable per variant, and losers are switched off in flight once each variant has a readable sample (Aaron, Apr 6: the second phase of testing is the value prop or pain point of the winning offer; Mar 9: deactivate the angles that don't get proper responses; `/experiment-design` owns the read). Then the CTA and delivery, with subject lines last as the lowest-leverage lever (doctrine §5 step 9, §9), and data source and segment tested in parallel throughout. Alongside, exploit and expand the list: scale the signal-test slices with the best positive-reply rate that do not lose on time-agreed replies per 1,000 first touches (a positive lift with no rise in time-agreed replies is farming interest, doctrine §4) back INTO the big campaigns, and introduce new data-point combinations. Because the catalog records every obtainable signal and the free fields that come with the rows ride along (the Collection Scope rule in `/data-point-discovery`; a paid signal column is collected only when the offer or a live test needs it), Wave 2 mostly mines **untested combinations of columns already in `tam.csv`**, plus any genuinely-new `in_reserve` point added at refresh.
- **Wave 3+: continuous.** Driven by `/cold-email-weekly-rhythm` + `/experiment-design`: keep mining untested slices, retire losers, test the next variable. **When the portfolio is thin, the default next move is more volume on the winner and its positioning variants** (widen its list through the `/tam-build` expansion-for-volume ladder while fit holds) **before a new signal slice** (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22).

**Volume adequacy: cold email is a volume game, so forecast it and anchor on the broad campaign (Eric).** Before locking the wave, forecast each campaign's real contact volume: `enriched companies in the slice × contacts-per-company × email-yield (≈50 to 70%)`. Two rules follow.
1. **The broad / value-prop campaign is the volume anchor and should be pushed to the 1,000 to 20,000 lead band the TAM allows** (high-volume default). The ≥500-company floor is launch *eligibility*, a minimum and never a volume target, so clearing it starts the job rather than finishing it. A healthy broad campaign is thousands to tens of thousands of leads (reference from a prior client build: one broad slice carried ~1,500 leads; the high-volume target grows that by scraping every ICP-matching decision-maker per company and by the `/tam-build` expansion-for-volume ladder). **Contacts are not the gate, companies are.** A signal slice can be legitimately thin on contacts, so a 50-to-200-contact why-now slice is fine **provided it spans ≥500 companies**, which is the same company-universe reading the floor above settles (817 companies yielding 396 verified contacts qualifies). What is never fine is a slice under 500 companies. Volume comes from the broad anchor plus the total across the wave, not from every slice being large. Do not fragment a big TAM into only small slices and skip the broad anchor.
2. **ICP-adequacy check (surface, do not paper over).** If the whole enriched TAM cannot produce a single ≥500-company campaign, or its × yield cannot give the broad anchor real volume AND clear the per-campaign raw-reply readability floor (~300 first-touch sends, `/cold-email-weekly-rhythm`; the pooled offer-validation flip is judged separately at its own ~1,300-contact floor, see the flip criterion above) on each launched campaign, the ICP is too small to be a volume game on its own. Under the whole-TAM first wave the check also fires when the qualified TAM cannot give each offer in the count its own ≥500-company cell or each arm its ~1,300 pooled first-touch contacts (the Offer-Validation Wave block above; Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22). That is an offer/ICP decision, not something to hide behind a pile of tiny slices: surface it and widen the lever the TAM can afford (all ICP job titles / the full buying committee, headcount band, geography, adjacent verticals, or the `/tam-build` expansion-for-volume ladder while ICP fit holds). **Shipping a sub-500-company campaign is an explicit operator exception to the HARD RULE above, never something this skill proposes on its own:** surface the shortfall and the widening options, and let the operator make that call. The standing example is a genuinely small Nordic niche client whose live campaigns sat well under 500; the directive surfaces cases like it rather than auto-acting on them. A big TAM sliced into many small campaigns is a process miss, not a small niche.

**Weak-data companies are not forced into a contrived signal campaign.** Covering volume means reaching every in-ICP company through a *real* campaign, never manufacturing fake signals to fill a roster. If a set of in-ICP companies carries no signal that clears the ≥500-company floor, do **not** contrive a thin signal campaign for them. Put them in the **broad campaign** (value-prop-led, no-signal or light AI personalization; see Section 2's No-AI campaign pattern), which is the wave's volume anchor and where they belong anyway. A clean broad campaign for relevant companies beats a forced niche campaign on weak data.

**Order the send by growth when the TAM outruns capacity (Eric; silent ordering, never a filter).** When the TAM is bigger than the warmed fleet can actually send to, the question is not who to delete. It is who to contact *first*. Order the send queue by growth signal: companies carrying headcount growth (`sales_headcount_growth_6mo`, `headcount_trend`) get contacted before flat or shrinking ones. Eric's reasoning is that there are broadly two kinds of companies, the ones trying to grow and the ones content with the status quo, and more growth means more problems to solve, so a growing company is likelier to need what you sell. Out of a 50,000-contact TAM, the first list he pulls is the ~5,000 whose company grew headcount 1%+ in six months. **Headcount is the workable growth signal today**; traffic growth would serve the same purpose, but nothing in the stack collects the history yet (the change-detection gap), so do not order against a traffic-growth column that isn't there.

Three constraints make this **ordering** rather than a filter, and they are the load-bearing part:
1. **It is deliberately NOT a TAM filter.** Eric narrows a big TAM for precision. The high-volume operator directive (2026-07-07, the newest volume authority) does the opposite: it widens the TAM (the `/tam-build` expansion-for-volume ladder) to reach 2,000 to 30,000 sends/day, expanding while ICP fit holds down to the ~50% rework floor. A growth pre-filter would shrink the very TAM this flow is straining to grow, so as an **exclusion** it fights that rule. As **ordering** it serves it. Nobody is deleted or excluded: flat and shrinking companies stay in the TAM and get contacted later in the queue.
2. **It is not buildable as a discovery filter anyway, so do not try to build it as one later.** Growth is generally sold as per-record enrichment on a list you already hold, not as a search-time filter at discovery, and a search-time growth filter is a premium tier where it exists at all. Check what the contract's company-discovery source actually offers before assuming otherwise, and treat this as mechanically an enrich-then-order pass over the built TAM, never a gate at discovery.
3. **It only pays when capacity is the binding constraint,** which the high-volume operator directive (2026-07-07) says it normally is (an account's real daily total sits well below a per-client 2,000 to 30,000 target until the warmed fleet is scaled to match). If the warmed fleet can cover the whole TAM, ordering changes nothing but the sequence. It costs nothing either way: no new columns and no new spend, because it reuses growth columns `/tam-build` already collects.

**The growth signal stays OUT of the copy.** Eric uses it silently and never mentions it in the email. This is the same targeting-filter-vs-copy-variable line the Message-differential split test draws above: growth here *selects the send order*, it does not change what you'd say. (A growth slice can still earn its own campaign on its own merits inside the bounded test allocation, per the Signal-Driven Slice Library's growth rows. The ordering pass itself stays invisible to the reader.) Rationale and the Eric source: `/ask-eric` (`eric-doctrine.md` section 8, the growth filter) (growth as a prioritization pass, not a TAM filter).

When there is no full scored TAM, stop and route to `/tam-build`, then `/lead-scoring`. Website research may shape ideas, but it cannot bypass the governed campaign-selection gate.

### Personalization tier: match the effort to the account's likely value

This is a light steer on top of the launch waves above, not a competing taxonomy. **Since 2026-09-21 the ladder climbs through what is delivered, not through per-contact copy (operator-approved Aaron alignment; Hard Rule 34 governs; doctrine §8, ledger R02, R13).** The Email 1 body is one shared text per campaign or offer-angle slice; the signal's first job is list relevance, and effort goes into the slice's relevance and the deliverable, never into stacked per-contact inserts. Each rung only earns its keep above a certain account value. The skill is matching the rung to the account, not pushing every account to the fanciest rung. The fancy asset is a multiplier on a working offer and a tight list, never the lever itself, so fix the offer and the slice first; add heavier effort only where the likely deal value pays for it. The earlier per-contact-body tiers are archived at `outbound/_archive/aaron-alignment-2026-09-21/campaign-strategy--personalization-tier.md`.

When `tam.csv` carries `deal_value_tier` (the free derived contract-value estimate from `/tam-build`), use it to set the depth per slice or account:

- **`low` tier (bulk of a volume list):** the shared slice body plus `{{first_name}}` and, where they sharpen the offer, at most two per-contact variables already in the data or cheaply derived, placed inside the offer sentence only, never as an opener and never as stacked AI lines: `sells_to_vertical` (who the prospect sells to), `service_offered` (the prospect's own product or service, a short phrase), or `own_best_client` (the prospect's own best client from their site, as the target of the deliverable). Each derived value carries a `_source` evidence URL, is derived by Claude Code sub-agents from the prospect's own site (`/personalization-subagent-pattern`, never an AI column in the list workbench), and rows missing a value get the fallback body (Aaron, Sep 21; Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes first-name-only per-contact variation, house HR34). A clean broad campaign with a strong offer beats over-personalizing a low-value account.
- **`mid` tier:** the shared slice body plus, where it changes what is delivered, a per-row deliverable variable, such as the case-study variable used as the target of the deliverable (a look-alike list shaped like the prospect's own best case study; doctrine §9, examples §1.5). The per-contact `email_N_x` columns (`/personalization-subagent-pattern`) carry that shared text; the mechanism is unchanged.
- **`high` tier (small, genuinely high-value named accounts):** the same shared slice body, and this is the only place a bespoke custom-asset motion (personalized landing page, report, mockup) would ever be justified, as the deliverable itself. Per-account custom assets are open for testing since 2026-09-22 (the premium-tier deferral is retired in `/lead-magnet-brainstorm`): a custom-asset offer runs as its own arm, judged on time-agreed replies, and passes the Artifact-Truth Gate. This skill still never invents one; it reads the offers `/lead-magnet-brainstorm` locked.

Honest caveat: `deal_value_tier` is an estimate from firmographics and only matters when the TAM has real spread in deal value. If the ICP is uniform (every account is roughly the same size), the tier is nearly constant: keep personalization depth consistent across the wave and do not manufacture a tiering distinction that the data does not support.

### Contact-signal campaigns outrank company-signal campaigns: HARD RULE (operator directive 2026-06-29)

A **contact-level** signal (a why-now about the *person*: new-in-seat decision-maker, promotion into a budget title, work-history context) is **higher-intent than a company-level** signal (a state of the *company*: running ads, hiring, billing-migration). So:

- **Build campaigns at BOTH levels, but rank contact-signal slices ABOVE company-signal slices** at equal viability. When capacity forces an order, a viable contact-signal campaign launches before a company-only one. (This generalizes the existing #1 priority "job change + work history" to *every* contact-signal slice.) The flagship is **`contact_new_in_seat`**, a decision-maker in their first ~12 months, collected at Layer-1 cost. This signal comes from the contact-discovery source's role-tenure filter (a job-duration or months-in-current-role field). Confirm the contract's contact-discovery source actually exposes that filter before building the slice, and check how it is scoped: some sources only expose tenure filtering against a supplied domain list rather than as a free search, which changes how the slice is assembled (`/data-point-discovery` Contact-discovery signal tier; pulled in `/email-waterfall` contact-list assembly). **Never assume the filter exists because a signal library names it.**
- **Company-span floor for a contact-signal slice (⚠ INTERPRETATION of the directive, flagged for operator correction).** The 2026-07-12 directive **retires the old ≥100-contact floor** and sets eligibility at ≥500 target companies, "the addressable company set the campaign targets". It does not spell out how that lands on a *contact*-signal slice such as `contact_new_in_seat`, so what follows is this skill's reading rather than a quoted rule. The only coherent one is that the slice's **company span** must clear the floor: the contacts carrying the signal must sit across **≥500 companies**. Count the distinct companies in the slice's contact pool from the Layer-1 contact pull (in the `provider-routing.yaml` `layers.people_discovery` order), **before any email reveal**, and test that count against 500. **There is no contacts floor any more**, so a slice spanning ≥500 companies qualifies even when it yields far fewer contacts, the same company-universe reading the prior-client case above settles. Under 500 companies of span, fold those contacts into a qualifying campaign, widen on the `/tam-build` expansion-for-volume ladder while ICP fit holds, or hold for a later wave; never ship it small, and keep the sub-threshold signal catalogued (it may cross 500 as the TAM grows). **Operator note:** this is the one place this skill extends the directive instead of quoting it, so if you meant a different reading (a contact-count target rather than a company-span test), correct this bullet.
- **Contacts carry both layers.** Because `/email-waterfall` joins company signals onto each contact (`co_*`) and tags the Tier-1 contact signals, the strongest slices **stack** a contact why-now with a company signal (e.g. `contact_new_in_seat = true AND co_billing_migration_signal = true`): the highest-intent predicate available. Prefer these stacked slices whenever the stacked predicate itself spans ≥500 companies; there is one floor now, tested on the stacked slice, not a separate floor per signal.

## Proof-Asset Gap Check (per planned angle)

When you plan an angle or slice, check whether it leans on a specific proof asset: a named client, a real case study, or an exact number/metric. If that proof is NOT already on file (`social-proof-bank.md`, `client-profile.yaml`, `offer-intelligence.md`, an approved artifact, or the intake), do not quietly assume the copywriter will fill it in or soften it later. Check `social-proof-bank.md` first: a segment-matched `proof_status = on_file` row means the proof is ready; a `gap_block` row for that segment means the gate should fire now. Flag the gap now and decide the tier on one question: is the angle hollow without this proof, or just less strong?

- BLOCK (hollow without it): the angle's whole credibility depends on the missing proof. Do not lock the proof-led angle until the proof exists. Emit the fill-in form (`proof-asset-request.md`) to the client and ship the slice now on an offer-led angle (or one the on-file evidence already supports); only the proof variant waits, and the proof line joins later as an A/B arm when it returns (Aaron process alignment, operator directive 2026-09-22; supersedes holding the slice until the proof returns, house proof-asset gate).

### Sourcing vs. Enriching Lists

When building campaigns, understand the difference:

**SOURCING** = Pulling a list of companies/people that ALL match a specific criteria
- Use when: The filter is a hard requirement (e.g., "only companies that raised Series A")
- Example: Source all companies that raised funding in last 6 months from Crunchbase

**ENRICHING** = Taking an existing list and adding data to filter/personalize
- Use when: The filter is one of many criteria, or you're personalizing an existing list
- Example: Take a list of SaaS companies and enrich with funding data, then filter for funded ones

**Key insight**: Enriching is often cheaper and more scalable than sourcing. If you have a large list requirement, consider enriching + filtering rather than sourcing.

## Creative Stretch Campaigns

Go beyond the obvious. Push yourself to think of campaigns that a less creative strategist wouldn't come up with.

### Techniques for Deeper Creativity:

**1. Quantify the Pain (the cost, not the raw count)**
- Use a headcount or department count to compute a COST, never to recite the number back at the prospect: "If each of the SDRs spends 5 hours/week researching, that's 50 hours/week of selling time lost." The implication is the line; the raw count is targeting input only. In copy, that cost figure rides in the PS as the email's single relevance point, never line one, because the offer opens the email (house rule, matching Aaron's opening-line swap, examples §1.1).
- Do NOT recite a scraped count at the prospect ("You have 10 SDRs") and do NOT name-drop individual employees ("I saw Mike, Laura, and James on your team"). Both read as surveillance, and the second also trips the observational-opener ban. Lead with what the number means, per `/campaign-copywriting` Signal-First Anti-Patterns and the Signal Angle Library (department-headcount caveat).

**2. Detect Unusual Job Titles**
- Some job titles signal perfect fit (e.g., "GTM Engineer" for a GTM tool)
- Look for titles unique to the client's space
- Target companies WITH that title, or companies WITHOUT it (both can work)

**3. Invert Signals (Use Carefully)**
- Instead of "has GTM Engineer," try "has VP Sales but NO GTM Engineer"
- The absence of something can signal opportunity

**IMPORTANT: Only invert signals that are reliably detectable.**
- Job titles are reliably detectable via LinkedIn.
- Job postings are reliably detectable.
- Do not use tech stack absence as a hard signal. If we do not detect a tool, that does not mean they do not use it.
- Do not use "No enrichment tool detected" as a campaign premise.

If you can't reliably prove the absence, don't build a campaign around it.

**4. Combine Multiple Signals**
- Stack 3-4 filters for hyper-niche lists
- Example: "New VP Sales + hiring SDRs + uses Salesforce + posted about scaling outbound"

**5. Estimate Hidden Data**
- Estimate what data matters to their team based on their business
- Suggest specific data points you could gather. Name the specific thing their SDRs likely need and explain that we can pull it automatically.

**6. Role-Specific Workflows**
- What does a day in the life look like for the target role?
- What manual tasks do they do that could be automated?
- What data do they wish they had but don't?

**7. Detect Team Structure**
- Count people in specific roles via LinkedIn
- Identify org structure (do they have RevOps? Dedicated SDR team? Sales Engineers?)
- Tailor messaging to their team composition
